REVIEW CONTENT
================================================================================

This paper presents a promising idea for joint user scheduling and discrete power control in a multi-cell OFDMA system by formulating the problem as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) program for Coherent Ising Machines (CIMs). The core concept of mapping inter-cell interference to quadratic penalties is elegant and highly relevant. However, the manuscript in its current form is severely underdeveloped and falls far short of the standards for a top-tier publication. It reads more like a proposal or an extended abstract than a full research paper.

The following are major areas that require substantial revision:

**1. Lack of Depth and Context:**
*   **Missing Sections:** The paper is critically missing standard sections, including a proper **Introduction** to motivate the problem, a comprehensive **Related Work** section to contextualize the contribution, a detailed **Experimental Evaluation** to validate the claims, and a **Discussion** of the results and limitations.
*   **Superficial Content:** All existing sections are extremely brief and lack necessary detail. The System Model omits specifics of the channel model. The QUBO Transformation glosses over the critical and non-trivial mapping of inequality constraints. The Results section is purely qualitative, offering no data, figures, or tables to support its claims.
*   **Word Count and References:** The paper is far too short and contains zero citations. A full research paper in this field is expected to be in the 5000-8000 word range and cite 15-20+ foundational and contemporary works.

**2. Methodological and Formulation Flaws:**
*   **Incorrect Power Budget Constraint:** The proposed QUBO formulation for the power budget, $\lambda_{\text{pow}} (\sum P_i x_i - P_b^{\max})^2$, is fundamentally incorrect for an inequality constraint ($\le$). This quadratic penalty equally punishes solutions for being *under* the power budget, which is a valid and often desirable outcome. The standard and correct method is to introduce binary slack variables to convert the inequality into an equality, which must be implemented and explained.
*   **Penalty Parameter Selection:** The paper hand-waves the selection of penalty parameters ($\lambda_{\text{1hot}}, \lambda_{\text{pow}}$) by stating they must be "large enough." This is insufficient. A principled discussion on how these crucial hyperparameters are scaled relative to the objective function's magnitude is necessary for reproducibility and robustness.

**3. Insufficient Experimental Rigor and Validation:**
*   **No Baselines:** The claims of effectiveness are unsubstantiated as the proposed method is not compared against any standard baseline algorithms (e.g., an interference-aware greedy scheduler, a cell-local scheduler).
*   **No Quantitative Results:** The manuscript lacks figures, tables, and any quantitative performance metrics. Claims like "closely matches a greedy water-filling baseline" must be rigorously supported with data, including mean performance, standard deviation, and/or confidence intervals over a statistically significant number of simulation runs.
*   **Missing Ablation Study:** The core contribution is the interference-aware coupling term. An ablation study comparing the full model to a variant without these terms is essential to demonstrate their importance empirically.
*   **Scalability Analysis:** The paper mentions scalability but provides no evidence. An experiment showing how performance scales with problem size (e.g., number of users) is needed.

**4. Code and Reproducibility:**
*   The provided Python code, while a good start, reflects the paper's flaws. It implements the incorrect power budget penalty, lacks baselines, and does not perform statistical analysis over multiple runs. It must be updated to support a rigorous experimental evaluation.
*   The reference to `simulation.py` in the main text is inappropriate for a formal publication and should be moved to a "Code Availability" statement.

**Decision:** The paper requires major revisions. The authors must fundamentally restructure and expand the manuscript, correct the flawed QUBO formulation, conduct a rigorous and comparative experimental evaluation, and properly situate their work within the scientific literature. The core idea is strong, but a complete rewrite is necessary to transform this sketch into a convincing scientific contribution.
