REVIEW CONTENT
================================================================================

This paper presents a promising approach for joint user scheduling and discrete power control in a multi-cell OFDMA system by formulating the problem as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) program and solving it with a Coherent Ising Machine (CIM) inspired annealer. The core idea of mapping interference as quadratic penalties in the QUBO is elegant and relevant for emerging physics-inspired computing hardware. However, the current manuscript is at a very early stage and falls significantly short of the standards required for a top-tier journal publication. It reads more like a brief abstract or a proposal than a full research paper.

The following are major areas that require substantial revision:

**1. Lack of Depth and Context:**
*   **Missing Sections:** The paper is critically missing standard sections, including a proper Introduction that motivates the problem, a comprehensive Related Work section that situates the work within the existing literature, a detailed Methodology/Experiments section, and a Discussion of the results and limitations.
*   **Superficial Content:** All existing sections are extremely brief. For instance, the System Model lacks detail on channel modeling, the QUBO Transformation section glosses over key derivations and the challenging mapping of inequality constraints, and the Results section presents a qualitative summary without any data, figures, or tables.
*   **Word Count and References:** The paper is severely underdeveloped, with a word count under 1000 words and zero citations. A full research paper is expected to be in the 5000-8000 word range with 15-20+ references to foundational and contemporary work.

**2. Methodological and Formulation Issues:**
*   **Incorrect Power Budget Constraint:** The QUBO formulation for the power budget constraint, $\lambda_{\text{pow}} (\sum P_i x_i - P_b^{\max})^2$, is incorrect for an inequality constraint ($\le$). This formulation penalizes solutions for being *under* the power budget, which is undesirable. The standard method is to introduce binary slack variables to convert the inequality into an equality, which should be implemented and explained.
*   **Lack of Justification for Penalty Parameters:** The paper mentions that "Large enough $\lambda_{\text{1hot}}$ and $\lambda_{\text{pow}}$ enforce feasibility" without any discussion on how these crucial hyperparameters are selected. A sensitivity analysis or at least a principled discussion on their scaling is necessary.

**3. Insufficient Experimental Rigor and Validation:**
*   **No Baselines:** The performance of the proposed method is not compared against any standard baseline algorithms (e.g., greedy schedulers, iterative water-filling, or even a simple random allocation). Without this comparison, the claims of effectiveness are unsubstantiated.
*   **No Quantitative Results:** The paper lacks figures, tables, and any quantitative performance metrics. Claims like "closely matches a greedy water-filling baseline" must be supported with data, including mean performance, standard deviation, and/or confidence intervals over multiple simulation runs (e.g., 100 random "drops").
*   **Missing Ablation Study:** The key contribution is the interference-aware coupling term. An ablation study comparing the full model to one without these terms would powerfully demonstrate their importance.
*   **Scalability Analysis:** While the paper mentions scalability, it does not provide evidence. An experiment showing how performance and runtime scale with the number of users, cells, or RBs is needed.

**4. Code and Reproducibility:**
*   The provided Python code is a good starting point but reflects the paper's shortcomings. It lacks implementations of baseline algorithms, does not perform statistical analysis over multiple runs, and contains the same flawed power budget penalty formulation as the paper. The code must be updated to support the rigorous experimental evaluation required.
*   The reference to `simulation.py` in the main text is inappropriate for a formal publication. This should be moved to a "Code Availability" statement or footnote.

In summary, the paper needs to be fundamentally restructured and massively expanded. The authors should build upon the current core idea by adding the necessary theoretical depth, formulating the QUBO constraints correctly, conducting a rigorous and comparative experimental evaluation, and situating their work properly within the scientific literature. The revisions should transform this manuscript from a preliminary sketch into a complete and convincing scientific contribution.
