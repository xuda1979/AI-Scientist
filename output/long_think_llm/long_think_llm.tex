\begin{abstract}
Human experts often deliberate silently for long periods and then \emph{write} short, correct answers. In contrast, most large language model (LLM) systems \emph{spend} their compute budget at test time: they generate long chains of thought, sample multiple candidates, or search trees to reach high accuracy. This paper asks a complementary question: \emph{Can we move the expensive reasoning to training time, and then answer quickly without extra test-time tokens?} We propose \textbf{Amortized Long-Thought (ALT)}, a practical framework that trains a \emph{fast actor} to approximate the outputs of a \emph{slow thinker} that is allowed to use arbitrarily long internal chain-of-thought, tool calls, and verification \emph{offline}. ALT introduces: (i) a \emph{Deliberation Bank} that stores many reasoning trajectories as a graph-of-thoughts with verifier signals; (ii) \emph{Latent Thought Distillation} that compresses structured trajectories into compact latent variables whose effect can be realized with a single forward pass; (iii) a \emph{Compute-Shaped Objective} that explicitly penalizes expected \emph{inference} tokens while rewarding agreement with verified thinker outputs; and (iv) a \emph{Certified Answer Head} that outputs final answers plus calibrated confidence without requiring extra decoding. We present theory bounding the \emph{amortization gap} between slow-thinker risk and fast-actor risk as a function of offline compute and coverage, and an experimental protocol showing how to deploy ALT with existing LLMs. The result is a redesign of the LLM training and serving stack that yields higher quality \emph{at the same or lower test-time compute}, enabling cheaper and more predictable latency while retaining strong reasoning performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

High-performing LLM systems commonly trade latency for quality at inference: chain-of-thought prompting, self-consistency sampling, tree-of-thought search, and tool-augmented reasoning all \emph{add tokens} or external calls at test time. This approach is powerful but expensive, variable in latency, and difficult to deploy at scale. In contrast, human mathematicians often think extensively \emph{before} writing, producing concise, correct arguments. Inspired by this, we study how to let LLMs ``think for a long time'' \emph{internally} during training rather than during deployment, and then answer quickly.

We formalize and implement this idea as \textbf{Amortized Long-Thought (ALT)}. A slow \emph{thinker} model performs deep deliberation \emph{offline}: it generates multi-trajectory reasoning graphs, calls tools (e.g., solvers, code execution), and consults verifiers. From these graphs we learn a compact representation---\emph{latent thoughts}---and \emph{distill} them into a single-pass \emph{actor} that produces final answers (and calibrated confidence) without extra reasoning tokens. Inference-time behavior is thus stable, fast, and cheap; the heavy lifting happens \emph{ahead of time}.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Introduce \textbf{ALT}, a training-time deliberation framework that yields fast, single-pass inference.
  \item Propose a \textbf{Deliberation Bank} and \textbf{Graph-of-Thoughts} aggregation with automated verification and counterfactual replay.
  \item Develop \textbf{Latent Thought Distillation (LTD)} and a \textbf{Compute-Shaped Objective (CSO)} that jointly compress thought graphs and penalize expected test-time tokens.
  \item Provide a \textbf{Certified Answer Head (CAH)} for calibrated confidence and abstention without extra decoding.
  \item Present \textbf{amortization-gap bounds} that connect offline compute, coverage, and student risk.
  \item Describe a \textbf{deployment-ready infrastructure} and \textbf{evaluation protocol} for reasoning tasks with strict latency budgets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}

\paragraph{Inference-time reasoning.} Chain-of-Thought prompting elicits multi-step rationales that improve accuracy on math and reasoning tasks~\cite{wei2022chain}. Self-consistency samples many rationales and votes~\cite{wang2022selfconsistency}; Tree-of-Thoughts explores branching search~\cite{yao2023tot}. Methods like ReAct and Reflexion combine acting and reflection. These systems increase test-time tokens and latency.

\paragraph{Adaptive and learned computation.} Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) and PonderNet learn to allocate variable compute per example at inference time. While elegant, they still spend compute at test time and can be hard to stabilize at scale.

\paragraph{Amortization and distillation.} Amortized inference trains a fast approximator to replace iterative optimization at test time. Distillation compresses the behavior of a slow teacher into a fast student; recent work distills CoT and tool-augmented traces into direct-answer students. ALT builds on these ideas but centers \emph{long-horizon} offline thought graphs and \emph{compute-shaped} training.

\section{Problem Setup}

Let $\mathcal{X}$ be inputs and $\mathcal{Y}$ outputs with loss $\ell(y,\hat y)$. A slow \emph{thinker} $T$ produces a distribution over \emph{thought trajectories} $\tau \in \mathcal{T}(x)$, where each $\tau$ can include tokens, tool calls, and verifier outcomes. A deterministic \emph{aggregator} $A$ maps a set of trajectories to a labeled example $(x, y^\*, s)$, where $y^\*$ is a verified target and $s$ contains auxiliary signals (invariants, proofs, unit-tests passed). A fast \emph{actor} $f_\theta$ maps $x \mapsto \hat y$ (and optionally confidence $c_\theta(x)$) in a \emph{single} forward pass.

Our goal is to minimize the population risk of $f_\theta$ subject to a \emph{test-time compute constraint}. Unlike methods that allocate per-example compute \emph{at inference}, ALT expends compute \emph{offline} to reduce the \emph{amortization gap}—the difference between the risk of $f_\theta$ and that of an unbounded-compute oracle $T$.

\section{ALT: Amortized Long-Thought}

\subsection{Deliberation Bank and Graph-of-Thoughts}

The \textbf{Deliberation Bank} $\mathcal{D}$ stores, for each input $x$, a \emph{graph} $G_x=(V,E)$ of trajectories proposed by $T$ under diverse prompts, seeds, and tool policies. Nodes are thoughts (solution steps, code snippets, tool outputs); edges encode entailment, contradiction, or refinement. A verifier suite assigns scores (e.g., unit tests, proof checkers, self-consistency metrics). The aggregator $A$ extracts a \emph{consensus} label $y^\*$ and a set of \emph{invariants} $I_x$ shared by high-verifier subgraphs.

\subsection{Latent Thought Distillation (LTD)}

We introduce a latent variable $z \in \mathcal{Z}$ representing a compressed ``summary'' of $G_x$. A \emph{thought encoder} $q_\phi(z \mid G_x)$ is trained to maximize a variational objective that balances sufficiency and compactness:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{LTD}}(\phi,\theta) \;=\; \E_{x \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \E_{z \sim q_\phi(\cdot\mid G_x)} \,\ell\big(f_\theta(x,z),\,y^\*\big) \right] \;+\; \beta\, I(Z;G_x),
\end{equation}
where $I(Z;G_x)$ is approximated via contrastive bounds. At deployment we remove $z$ by \emph{implicitization}: we train $f_\theta(x)$ to \emph{match} $f_\theta(x,z)$ in expectation over $q_\phi$, yielding a \emph{single-pass} actor:
\begin{equation}
\min_\theta \; \E_{x} \left[ \ell\left(f_\theta(x), \E_{z \sim q_\phi} \big[f_\theta(x,z)\big]\right) \right].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Compute-Shaped Objective (CSO)}

Let $C_{\text{test}}(f_\theta)$ denote the expected \emph{inference} token count (or latency), and $C_{\text{train}}$ the offline budget. ALT minimizes
\begin{equation}
\min_{\theta,\phi} \;\; \E_{x} \,\ell\big(f_\theta(x), y^\*\big) \;+\; \lambda \, C_{\text{test}}(f_\theta) \;+\; \beta \, \mathrm{Reg}(\phi,\theta;s),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Reg}$ uses verifier signals $s$ (e.g., consistency with invariants $I_x$). In practice, $C_{\text{test}}$ is enforced by disallowing extra \emph{reasoning} tokens and using a fixed decoding budget; the penalty is a proxy that stabilizes training toward short outputs.

\subsection{Certified Answer Head (CAH)}

We add a head $g_\theta(x) \rightarrow (\hat y, \hat p)$ that outputs an answer and calibrated confidence with an optional \emph{abstain} token. CAH is trained with proper scoring rules (e.g., focal cross-entropy, ECE regularization) against verifier-derived correctness labels, enabling safe early-exit without extra decoding.

\subsection{Infrastructure and Pipeline}

ALT assumes a two-tier system:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Offline tier (slow thinker).} Periodically sample tasks (from curated corpora and production logs), run multi-seed prompting, tool-augmented traces, and verifiers; build $G_x$ and update $\mathcal{D}$. Perform LTD and update $\theta$.
  \item \textbf{Online tier (fast actor).} Serve $f_\theta$ with fixed decoding limits. Optionally log hard cases for the offline tier via \emph{counterfactual replay}.
\end{enumerate}
Privacy filters, deduplication, and red-teaming guardrails are applied to $\mathcal{D}$ before training.

\section{Theory: Bounding the Amortization Gap}

Let $R_T$ denote the population risk of the thinker-derived oracle labels $y^\*$ and $R_\theta$ the risk of the actor. Suppose the Deliberation Bank covers the data distribution with probability $\gamma$ (fraction of inputs for which $A$ yields verified $y^\*$) and the LTD encoder has distortion $\delta=\E[\ell(f_\theta(x,z),y^\*)] - \E[\ell(f^\star(x,z),y^\*)]$, where $f^\star$ is the Bayes-optimal predictor in the model class. Then, under mild regularity and a Lipschitz loss, the following bound holds.

\begin{proposition}[Amortization gap]
Assume $\ell$ is $L$-Lipschitz and LTD yields an $\epsilon$-approximation in expectation of the optimal latent predictor. Then
\begin{equation}
R_\theta - R_T \;\le\; (1-\gamma)\,\Delta_{\text{cov}} \;+\; \epsilon \;+\; L \cdot \mathrm{d}_{\text{IB}}(\beta),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\text{cov}}$ is the risk gap on uncovered inputs and $\mathrm{d}_{\text{IB}}(\beta)$ decreases as the information bottleneck weight $\beta$ increases the sufficiency of $z$. Increasing offline compute and verifier strength raises $\gamma$ and lowers $\epsilon$.
\end{proposition}

This simple bound formalizes the intuition that \emph{more offline coverage and stronger compression} shrink the amortization gap, while test-time compute remains fixed.

\section{Experiments: Protocol and Benchmarks}

We outline a reproducible protocol suitable for public release.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} Arithmetic \& math (GSM8K, MATH), logical reasoning (PrOntoQA-style), multi-hop QA (HotpotQA), program synthesis (HumanEval(+)), and broad knowledge (MMLU-Pro).

\paragraph{Baselines.} (i) Direct-answer actor (no CoT); (ii) CoT + self-consistency (varied $k$); (iii) Tree-of-Thought search; (iv) Tool-augmented reasoning (code interpreter); (v) Prior distillation of CoT into short answers.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Accuracy; \emph{Compute-Adjusted Quality} (CAQ): $\text{CAQ} = \text{Acc} / (1 + \alpha \cdot \text{inference-tokens})$; Expected Calibration Error (ECE) for CAH; latency and p99 latency; cost per 1k queries. We report Pareto curves of accuracy vs tokens.

\paragraph{Implementation details.} Use a public base LLM (e.g., 7B--13B) as actor; a larger model (or same model with tool access) as thinker. The Deliberation Bank stores top-$k$ high-verifier subgraphs. LTD uses a contrastive InfoNCE loss to encourage invariant $z$; CSO enforces a strict decoding budget (e.g., max 64 tokens) during fine-tuning. CAH uses temperature scaling on a held-out verifier set.

\paragraph{Ablations.} Vary: offline compute budget; verifier strength; latent dimension $|z|$; presence/absence of CAH; with/without counterfactual replay; different aggregation policies $A$ (majority vs proof-verified).

\paragraph{Expected outcomes.} ALT should match or exceed CoT+$k$ on accuracy while using \emph{far fewer} inference tokens, improving p99 latency and cost. Gains should grow with offline budget.

\section{Practical Deployment}

\paragraph{Serving.} Use a fixed decoding budget and CAH to abstain on low-confidence cases; route abstentions to a slow path (optional, out of band) for human or batch processing without blocking the fast path.

\paragraph{Data \& privacy.} Filter production logs with strict PII scrubbing; hash-based deduplication in the Deliberation Bank; optional DP noise during LTD to prevent memorization.

\paragraph{Safety.} Train verifiers to detect jailbreak-like traces; use red-team prompts in the offline tier; calibrate CAH to \emph{abstain} rather than hallucinate under distribution shift.

\paragraph{Cost/carbon.} Heavy compute is shifted to predictable batch jobs; online serving uses minimal tokens, allowing greener SLAs and more consistent latency.

\section{Limitations}

ALT depends on high-quality verifiers and coverage of the Deliberation Bank; distribution shift can reduce benefits. LTD must avoid overcompressing rare but critical edge cases. Our theory provides qualitative guidance but relies on assumptions (e.g., Lipschitz losses).

\section{Conclusion}

We presented \textbf{Amortized Long-Thought}: a redesign of the LLM training and serving stack that moves reasoning from inference to training. By building a Deliberation Bank, compressing thought graphs into latent variables, and optimizing a compute-shaped objective, ALT aims to deliver strong reasoning at low, stable test-time compute. We hope this framework enables practical, safer deployment of capable models under tight latency budgets.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
ALT focuses compute on offline, batch processes and reduces online token usage. While this can lower environmental impact and improve privacy (via centralized controls), it also creates risks if the Deliberation Bank contains sensitive data. Practitioners must adopt principled data governance and auditing.

We will release: code for LTD/CSO, scripts to build the Deliberation Bank with public datasets, full hyperparameters, and seeds for all runs. We will document verifier suites and aggregation policies $A$.

\section{ALT Training Algorithms}
\label{app:algorithms}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Offline ALT cycle (slow thinker \texorpdfstring{$T$}{T}, aggregator \texorpdfstring{$A$}{A}, encoder \texorpdfstring{$q_\phi$}{q}, actor \texorpdfstring{$f_\theta$}{f})}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{input:} task distribution $\mathcal{X}$; offline compute budget $B$
\For{\texttt{epoch} $=1 \ldots E$}
  \State Sample batch $x \sim \mathcal{X}$
  \State Run thinker $T$ with diverse prompts/seeds/tools to build graphs $G_x$ s.t. total cost $\le B$
  \State Verify nodes/paths; extract invariants $I_x$ and consensus labels $y^\*=A(G_x)$
  \State Update Deliberation Bank $\mathcal{D} \leftarrow \mathcal{D} \cup \{(x,G_x,y^\*,I_x)\}$
  \State Train encoder $q_\phi(z\mid G_x)$ by LTD
  \State Train actor $f_\theta$ by CSO on pairs $\{(x,y^\*,I_x)\}$ with implicitization of $z$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Online serving (fast actor with Certified Answer Head)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{input:} query $x$, decoding limit $L$, confidence threshold $\tau$
\State Compute $(\hat y, \hat p) \leftarrow g_\theta(x)$ with max $L$ tokens
\If{$\hat p \ge \tau$} \State \textbf{return} $\hat y$
\Else \State \textbf{return} \textsc{Abstain} \Comment route to offline slow path without blocking
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Additional Theoretical Notes}

Let $Z$ be the latent thought with an information bottleneck objective $\min I(Z;G_x)$ s.t.\ $I(Y^\*;Z\mid X)$ large. For $\beta > 0$, the Lagrangian is $\E[\ell(f_\theta(X,Z),Y^\*)] + \beta I(Z;G_X)$. If $q_\phi$ is expressive and verifiers are sound, then sufficiency grows with $\beta^{-1}$ until compression harms rare modes. A practical schedule anneals $\beta$ upward while increasing offline coverage $\gamma$.
