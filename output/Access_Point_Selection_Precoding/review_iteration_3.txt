**Overall Assessment:**
The paper presents an innovative approach to joint access point (AP) selection and precoding in cell-free massive MIMO systems using a two-level framework involving Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) and a Coherent Ising Machine (CIM). While the core idea is promising, the manuscript is critically underdeveloped and lacks the necessary depth and structure to meet the standards of a top-tier journal. Significant expansion and restructuring are required.

**Major Concerns:**

1. **Lack of Structure and Content:** The paper is missing several standard sections of a research article, including a comprehensive Introduction, Related Work, detailed Methodology, comprehensive Experimental Evaluation, and a Discussion/Conclusion. The existing sections are brief and lack the necessary detail to be understandable or reproducible.

2. **Insufficient Literature Review:** The paper does not adequately situate the work within the current state-of-the-art. A thorough literature review is essential to discuss prior work on AP selection in cell-free systems and the emerging application of QUBO/Ising models in wireless communications. A minimum of 15-20 references is expected.

3. **Absence of Experimental Rigor:**
   - **No Baselines:** The paper claims a "5–20%" improvement over greedy selection but provides no definition of the baseline, no implementation, and no comparative data. A robust evaluation requires comparison against multiple well-established baselines.
   - **No Results Presented:** There are no figures, tables, or quantitative results within the paper itself. The simulation output shows a sum spectral efficiency of 0.001 b/s/Hz, which is physically impossible and points to a severe bug in the simulation.
   - **No Statistical Analysis:** The methodology should involve averaging results over many random channel realizations to report mean performance with measures of variance (e.g., standard deviation or confidence intervals shown as error bars).

4. **Methodological Opacity:** The description of the QUBO formulation is too high-level. Key details are missing, such as the justification for the interference coupling term, the specific implementation of the penalty functions, the choice of penalty parameters, and the details of the simulated CIM solver.

5. **Critically Flawed Simulation:** A review of the simulation code reveals multiple serious bugs:
   - **Incorrect SINR Evaluation:** The `evaluate_selection` function calculates interference using the sparse effective channel matrix `H_A`. Interference propagates through the true physical channel `H`, so this is incorrect and is the primary cause of the near-zero SINR results. The precoder should be designed based on the selected links (`H_A`), but its performance must be evaluated on the complete physical system (`H`).
   - **Flawed Effective Channel Construction:** The method for creating `H_A` is convoluted. The standard approach is a simple element-wise product `H_A = H * A`, where `A` is the selection matrix.
   - **Incorrect Constraint Penalty:** The user constraint penalty implementation in `build_qubo` is non-standard and incorrect for a quadratic penalty. It should be expanded from $(\sum x_i - L)^2$.

6. **No Complexity or Limitation Analysis:** The paper fails to analyze the computational complexity of the QUBO construction and solving process. It also omits any discussion of the limitations of the approach, such as the approximation quality of the QUBO model, the impact of CSI errors, or fronthaul requirements.

**Recommendation:** Reject and Resubmit.

The paper should be rejected in its current form. However, the core idea is valuable. The authors are strongly encouraged to perform a complete rewrite, addressing all the points above. This includes restructuring the paper, adding a comprehensive literature review (15-20+ citations), providing a detailed methodology, thoroughly debugging and extending the simulation code, conducting a rigorous experimental study with strong baselines and statistical analysis, and adding a discussion on complexity and limitations. To significantly increase the contribution, we also recommend exploring the flexibility of the QUBO framework by designing an objective that targets user fairness, a key concern in cell-free systems.
