\title{Traffic-Aware Massive MIMO Parameter Optimization with QUBO and Coherent Ising Machines}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Massive MIMO radio access networks expose discrete, strongly coupled degrees of freedom---the number of active antenna elements, the vertical downtilt angle, and the pilot reuse factor---that must be tuned dynamically to track spatiotemporal traffic while controlling inter-cell interference and power consumption. We propose a physics-inspired optimization framework that maps the joint, multi-cell configuration problem to a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) whose coefficients encode both local utilities and pairwise couplings derived from large-scale link-budget models. The resulting QUBO is solved by simulated annealing, a classical heuristic rooted in the same statistical mechanics as coherent Ising machines (CIMs). We provide a reproducible derivation of diagonal and off-diagonal coefficients using a baseline--delta inclusion--exclusion method, implement an end-to-end simulator for a macro-cell hexagonal deployment, and evaluate under uniform and hotspot traffic. Relative to static and greedy baselines, and to a cell-independent QUBO that ignores inter-cell couplings, the coordinated QUBO optimizer improves sum-rate by up to 30\%, more than doubles the 5th percentile user rate, and yields superior energy efficiency. The approach is compatible with Self-Organizing Network (SON) loops and scales by exploiting sparsity and locality in the QUBO matrix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Massive MIMO has redefined cellular architecture by leveraging large antenna arrays to achieve dramatic gains in spectral and energy efficiency \cite{marzetta2010noncooperative,bjornson2017massive,sanguinetti2019massive}. Realizing these gains operationally requires continuously adjusting discrete configuration parameters that strongly interact via interference: the active antenna count $M_b$, electrical downtilt $\theta_b$, and pilot reuse factor $\rho_b$ in sector $b$. Increasing $M_b$ enhances beamforming gain but raises energy consumption and, in some regimes, inter-cell interference \cite{ngo2013energy}. Changing $\theta_b$ reshapes the vertical radiation pattern, trading local coverage against neighbor interference footprints \cite{3gpp36814,3gpp38901}. The pilot reuse factor $\rho_b$ controls pilot contamination---a fundamental impairment in massive MIMO \cite{ali2015downlink,ashikhmin2012pilot}---but reduces orthogonal pilot availability.

Jointly selecting $(M_b,\theta_b,\rho_b)_{b=1}^B$ is a discrete, non-convex optimization with a rugged energy landscape. Classic SON techniques (heuristic rules, per-cell greedy updates) often converge slowly and can become trapped in poor equilibria under heterogeneous traffic \cite{hamdi2010self}. Learning-based approaches (e.g., deep or multi-agent RL) are promising but can be data-hungry, brittle to non-stationarity, and difficult to interpret or constrain \cite{elsayed2022deep}. We advocate a complementary path: formulate the task as a QUBO that captures pairwise interactions explicitly, and solve it with Ising-inspired heuristics \cite{lucas2014ising,mcmahon2016fully}. This mapping lets us design structured couplings: harmful parameter combinations across neighboring cells incur positive penalties, while beneficial combinations receive negative couplings. The result is a globally coordinated configuration computed quickly on commodity hardware, and conceptually aligned with CIMs and quantum annealers \cite{kadowaki1998quantum}.

Contributions:
- We pose multi-cell massive MIMO parameter tuning as a single QUBO whose structure captures capacity, coverage, energy, and pilot contamination. We derive concrete expressions for diagonal and off-diagonal coefficients grounded in 3GPP-compliant link-budget models \cite{3gpp36814,3gpp38901}.
- We present a constructive derivation for quadratic couplings from non-linear SINR utilities via baseline--delta inclusion--exclusion and local Taylor expansions, yielding reproducible QUBO coefficients that reflect interference physics.
- We implement a simulator generating user distributions, link gains, QUBO matrices, and solving via simulated annealing. We evaluate on uniform and hotspot traffic; compare against static, greedy, and a cell-independent QUBO (no inter-cell couplings); and run ablations isolating utility components.
- We analyze computational complexity and scalability, highlighting sparsity and locality in couplings and paths to accelerators \cite{calvanese2020quantum,chen2021hybrid,bian2019mapping}.

\section{Related Work}
Parameter optimization in cellular networks evolved from rule-based SON \cite{hamdi2010self} to learning-based methods \cite{elsayed2022deep}. Tilt optimization, power control, and antenna activation have been treated in convex frameworks under simplifying assumptions \cite{razaviyayn2013unified} and game-theoretic models \cite{giupponi2012survey}. Massive MIMO introduces unique couplings: array size influences both signal and interference; downtilt modifies vertical isolation; pilot reuse determines contamination \cite{marzetta2010noncooperative,ali2015downlink,ashikhmin2012pilot,hoydis2013massive,adhikary2013joint,sanguinetti2019massive}. QUBO/Ising methods provide a unifying substrate for pairwise interactions and physics-inspired solvers \cite{lucas2014ising,mcmahon2016fully,kadowaki1998quantum,glover1989tabu,inuma2020optimizing}. Wireless applications include user association, spectrum allocation, and beam selection \cite{calvanese2020quantum,bian2019mapping,chen2021hybrid}. Our work contributes a detailed QUBO construction for operational massive MIMO parameter tuning with explicit inter-cell and pilot couplings, validated against a cell-independent QUBO baseline to isolate coordination benefits.

\section{System Model and Utility}
We consider $B$ sectors at locations $\{\bm{r}_b\}$ in a macro deployment (hexagonal grid, \SI{500}{m} ISD). Users are distributed according to a traffic map with uniform and hotspot scenarios. Each sector $b$ selects parameters from discrete sets: $M_b \in \mathcal{M}$ (e.g., $\{32,64,128\}$), $\theta_b \in \Theta$ (e.g., $\{6^\circ,10^\circ,14^\circ\}$), and $\rho_b \in \mathcal{R}$ (e.g., $\{1,3\}$), reflecting hardware and operational quantization.

Large-scale pathloss follows 3GPP UMa \cite{3gpp36814,3gpp38901}. The vertical antenna pattern in dB is
\begin{equation}
A_v(\Delta\psi) = -\min\left\{12\left(\frac{\Delta\psi}{\psi_{3\text{dB}}}\right)^2, A_{\max}\right\},
\end{equation}
with $\Delta\psi$ elevation offset from the downtilted main lobe. The large-scale gain from sector $b$ to user $u$ is
\begin{equation}
G_{b,u}(M_b,\theta_b) = M_b \cdot L_{b,u} \cdot G_{\mathrm{ant}}(\phi_{b,u},\psi_{b,u};\theta_b),
\end{equation}
where $L_{b,u}$ captures distance-dependent loss and shadowing; the linear $M_b$ dependence approximates coherent array gain \cite{marzetta2010noncooperative,ngo2013energy}. Users associate to the sector with maximum large-scale gain.

For user $u$ served by $b$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SINR}_{b,u}=\frac{P_b G_{b,u}(M_b,\theta_b)}{N_0 W + \sum_{b'\neq b} P_{b'} G_{b',u}(M_{b'},\theta_{b'}) + I_{\mathrm{pilot}}(u)},
\end{equation}
with $I_{\mathrm{pilot}}$ modeling contamination under $\rho$ via gains from sectors in the same reuse class \cite{ashikhmin2012pilot,ali2015downlink}.

We maximize a weighted sum utility across sectors:
\begin{align}
U_b &= \alpha C_b - \beta E_b - \gamma V_b, \\
C_b &= \lambda_b \sum_{u\in \mathcal{A}_b} \eta_{\mathrm{OH}}(\rho_b)\log_2\big(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{b,u}\big),\\
E_b &= E_0 + \kappa_M M_b,\quad V_b = \sum_{u\in \mathcal{A}_b} \1(\mathrm{SINR}_{b,u}<\tau),
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{A}_b$ is the serving area, $\eta_{\mathrm{OH}}$ accounts for pilot overhead, $E_0$ static power, $\kappa_M$ per-RF-chain power, and $\tau$ a coverage threshold. Scalars $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ encode operator policy. The global objective maximizes $\sum_b U_b$.

\section{QUBO Construction}
We encode discrete selections with one-hot binaries: $x^{(M)}_{b,m}=1$ iff $M_b=M^{(m)}$, likewise $x^{(\theta)}_{b,t}$ and $x^{(\rho)}_{b,r}$. Let $\bm{x}\in\{0,1\}^N$ stack all binaries ($N=B(|\mathcal{M}|+|\Theta|+|\mathcal{R}|)$). The QUBO energy is
\begin{equation}
E(\bm{x}) = \bm{x}^\top \bm{Q}\bm{x} = \sum_i Q_{ii}x_i + \sum_{i<j}Q_{ij}x_ix_j,
\end{equation}
which we minimize. One-hot feasibility is enforced with penalties:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{1hot}}=\Lambda\sum_b\Big[(\sum_m x^{(M)}_{b,m}-1)^2+(\sum_t x^{(\theta)}_{b,t}-1)^2+(\sum_r x^{(\rho)}_{b,r}-1)^2\Big].
\end{equation}
Expanding adds $-\Lambda$ to diagonals and $+2\Lambda$ among pairs within each parameter set.

\subsection{Baseline--Delta Inclusion--Exclusion}
Directly expanding $-\sum_b U_b$ yields higher-order terms due to non-linear SINR coupling. We obtain a quadratic surrogate by constructing a baseline $\bm{x}_0$ (mid-range $M,\theta$, minimal $\rho$) and defining:
\begin{align}
\Delta U_i &= U(\bm{x}_0 \cup \{x_i{=}1\}) - U(\bm{x}_0), \\
\Delta U_{ij} &= U(\bm{x}_0 \cup \{x_i{=}1,x_j{=}1\}) - U(\bm{x}_0) - \Delta U_i - \Delta U_j.
\end{align}
Set $Q_{ii}=-\Delta U_i$ and $Q_{ij}=-\Delta U_{ij}$. This inclusion--exclusion construction \cite{lucas2014ising} captures:
- Intra-cell interactions: e.g., the benefit of larger $M_b$ depends on $\theta_b$.
- Inter-cell interference: e.g., selecting larger $M_b$ in $b$ increases interference to $b'$’s users; the harm depends on $\theta_{b'}$ and victim geometry.
- Pilot contamination: pairs of sectors in the same reuse class incur extra penalties.

To compute $\Delta U$ efficiently, we approximate capacity changes by Taylor expansions of $\log(1+z)$ and evaluate SINR with large-scale gains. For inter-cell coupling, the change in $b'$’s utility due to $M_b{=}M^{(m)}$ (interferer) and $\theta_{b'}{=}\theta^{(t)}$ (victim) is
\begin{align}
\Delta U_{b\to b'} &\approx \alpha \sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{b'}} \left[\log_2\left(1+\frac{S_{b',u}(t)}{N_0W+I_u+\Delta I_{b\to u}(m)}\right)\right. \nonumber\\
&\left.-\log_2\left(1+\frac{S_{b',u}(t)}{N_0W+I_u}\right)\right] - \gamma \Delta V_{b'},
\end{align}
where $S_{b',u}(t)$ is victim signal with tilt $t$, $I_u$ aggregate baseline interference, $\Delta I_{b\to u}(m)$ the interferer change for choice $m$, and $\Delta V_{b'}$ the change in coverage violations. The coupling $Q_{ij}$ between $x^{(M)}_{b,m}$ and $x^{(\theta)}_{b',t}$ is $-\Delta U_{b\to b'}$.

\subsection{Worked Example}
Consider neighbors $b$ (interferer) and $b'$ (victim). Let $x_i=x^{(M)}_{b,m}$ and $x_j=x^{(\theta)}_{b',t}$. For users $\mathcal{U}_{b'}$ associated to $b'$ at baseline, compute baseline signal $S^{\mathrm{base}}_{b',u}$ and interference $I^{\mathrm{base}}_u$. With $x_i$ active, the interferer contribution becomes $I^{\mathrm{new}}_{b\to u}$; with $x_j$ active, the victim signal is $S^{\mathrm{new}}_{b',u}(t)$. The interaction delta beyond additivity,
\begin{align}
\Delta U_{ij} &\approx \alpha \sum_{u\in \mathcal{U}_{b'}} \left[\log_2\frac{N_0W+I^{\mathrm{base}}_u+S^{\mathrm{new}}_{b',u}(t)}{N_0W+I^{\mathrm{new}}_{b\to u}+S^{\mathrm{new}}_{b',u}(t)}\right.\nonumber\\
&\left.- \log_2\frac{N_0W+I^{\mathrm{base}}_u+S^{\mathrm{base}}_{b',u}}{N_0W+I^{\mathrm{new}}_{b\to u}+S^{\mathrm{base}}_{b',u}}\right] - \gamma \Delta V_{b'},
\end{align}
becomes positive when this pair worsens $b'$’s KPIs, thus $Q_{ij}=-\Delta U_{ij}>0$, discouraging such combinations.

\subsection{Ising Mapping and Sparsity}
Binary $x\in\{0,1\}$ can be mapped to spins $s\in\{-1,1\}$ via $x=(1+s)/2$, yielding an Ising Hamiltonian with fields $h$ and couplings $J$ \cite{lucas2014ising}. Our QUBO remains sparse: significant inter-cell couplings occur only among neighbors due to pathloss; thus the off-diagonal structure reflects the network graph.

\section{Solver and Algorithm}
We employ simulated annealing (SA) \cite{kadowaki1998quantum,glover1989tabu}, performing one-hot moves within a sector and accepting uphill steps with probability $\exp(-\Delta E/T)$ under a cooling schedule. SA is simple, effective for modest QUBO sizes, and conceptually aligned with CIMs \cite{mcmahon2016fully}. The overall pipeline:

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Traffic-Aware Massive MIMO Optimization via QUBO}
\label{alg:qubo}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Input: Sector coordinates, user map, parameter sets $\mathcal{M},\Theta,\mathcal{R}$; weights $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$; penalty $\Lambda$.
\State Precompute large-scale gains $G_{b,u}(M,\theta)$ for all $(M,\theta)$.
\State Choose baseline $\bm{x}_0$ (mid-range $M,\theta$, minimal $\rho$).
\For{each variable $x_i$}
  \State Compute $\Delta U_i$; set $Q_{ii}\gets -\Delta U_i$.
\EndFor
\For{each pair $(x_i,x_j)$ with spatial relevance}
  \State Compute $\Delta U_{ij}$; set $Q_{ij}\gets -\Delta U_{ij}$.
\EndFor
\State Add one-hot penalties to $\bm{Q}$.
\State Solve $\min \bm{x}^\top \bm{Q}\bm{x}$ with SA using one-hot neighborhood moves.
\State Decode $\bm{x}$ to $(\bm{M},\bm{\theta},\bm{\rho})$; evaluate KPIs.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Methodology}
We simulate $B{=}7$ macro sectors on a hexagonal grid (ISD \SI{500}{m}), $W{=}\SI{20}{MHz}$, with large-scale UMa pathloss \cite{3gpp36814}. Two traffic scenarios:
- Uniform: users uniformly distributed.
- Hotspot: 60\% of users concentrated within a disk overlapping two adjacent cells.

Parameter sets: $M\in\{32,64,128\}$, $\theta\in\{6^\circ,10^\circ,14^\circ\}$, $\rho\in\{1,3\}$; hence $18$ choices per cell and $N{=}56$ binaries. Utility weights $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(1.0,0.05,1.0)$; one-hot penalty $\Lambda$ ensures feasibility.

Baselines:
- Static-Max M: $(M{=}128,\theta{=}10^\circ,\rho{=}1)$ across all cells.
- Greedy: iterate per-cell updates to maximize a local utility proxy given current neighbors (3 rounds).
- QUBO-SA (Cell-Independent): same pipeline but all inter-cell couplings set to zero (only intra-cell interactions and penalties).
- QUBO-SA (Coordinated): full proposed model with couplings.

KPIs over 20 Monte Carlo seeds:
- Sum-rate (Gbps): $\sum_u \log_2(1+\mathrm{SINR}_u)$ scaled by $W$.
- 5th percentile user rate (Mbps).
- Network power (W): $P_{\text{net}}=\sum_b (P_0+\kappa_M M_b)$ (representative values).
- Energy efficiency (Mbps/W): total Mbps divided by $P_{\text{net}}$.

We evaluate Max-Rate ($\beta{=}0$) and No-Coverage ($\gamma{=}0$) variants under hotspot traffic (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Max-Rate marginally increases sum-rate but reduces energy efficiency; No-Coverage harms cell-edge users. The full model best balances performance objectives. We also varied $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ and observed smooth trade-offs: higher $\beta$ reduces $M_b$ usage and power; higher $\gamma$ increases protective downtilts. \section{Complexity and Scalability}
Let $N=B(|\mathcal{M}|+|\Theta|+|\mathcal{R}|)$ be the number of binaries. In the worst case, pairwise couplings scale as $O(N^2)$; however, sparsity arises from locality: only neighboring cells contribute significant couplings, yielding near-linear scaling in $B$ up to parameter constants. QUBO construction is embarrassingly parallel and supports incremental updates as traffic shifts. SA runtime scales with steps and $N$; for $N\approx 56$, convergence occurs in milliseconds to seconds on CPU, compatible with minute-scale SON loops. Physics-inspired accelerators (CIMs, annealers) promise further speedups \cite{mcmahon2016fully,calvanese2020quantum}.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Explicit inter-cell couplings are essential: a cell-independent QUBO performs notably worse. The framework integrates with operational SON pipelines: traffic heatmaps inform QUBO construction; solvers return recommended $(M,\theta,\rho)$; operators tune $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ to reflect policy. Limitations include model mismatch (large-scale statistics vs. full channel dynamics) and hyperparameter tuning. SON typically optimizes long-term KPIs \cite{3gpp36814,3gpp38901}, making our abstractions appropriate. Future work includes ray-tracing validation, online QUBO updates, adding azimuth and power control, and hardware-in-the-loop demonstrations \cite{chen2021hybrid,bian2019mapping}.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a QUBO formulation for globally coordinated, traffic-aware massive MIMO parameter tuning with explicit interference and pilot coupling, solved by simulated annealing. The approach yields strong gains in capacity, cell-edge performance, and energy efficiency over static, greedy, and cell-independent QUBO baselines, while remaining compatible with SON time scales. The physics-inspired optimization paradigm offers a promising pathway to robust, interpretable, and scalable RAN automation.

import argparse, math, random
import numpy as np
import matplotlib as mpl
mpl.use('Agg')  # headless-safe backend
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# ------------------ Utilities ------------------

def db2lin(x_db: float) -> float:
    return 10**(x_db/10.0)

def lin2db(x_lin: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    return 10*np.log10(np.maximum(x_lin, 1e-15))

def seed_all(s: int):
    random.seed(s)
    np.random.seed(s)

# ------------------ Geometry and Users ------------------

def _hex_axial_to_xy(q: int, r: int, spacing_km: float):
    # Pointy-topped hex axial coordinate to 2D Cartesian
    x = spacing_km * (np.sqrt(3) * (q + r/2))
    y = spacing_km * (1.5 * r)
    return x, y

def hex_grid(Bside=1, spacing_km=0.5):
    """
    Returns coordinates for a hexagonal layout with given side length Bside.
    Number of cells = 1 + 3*Bside*(Bside+1). Center at (0,0).
    """
    coords = [(0.0, 0.0)]
    if Bside <= 0:
        return np.array(coords, dtype=float)
    # Axial neighbor directions (pointy-top)
    dirs = [(1, 0), (1, -1), (0, -1), (-1, 0), (-1, 1), (0, 1)]
    for r in range(1, Bside+1):
        q, s = r, 0  # start at (r,0)
        # walk the ring
        for d in range(6):
            dq, ds = dirs[d]
            for _ in range(r):
                x, y = _hex_axial_to_xy(q, s, spacing_km)
                coords.append((x, y))
                q += dq
                s += ds
    return np.array(coords, dtype=float)

def min_Bside_for_B(B: int) -> int:
    """Small helper to find minimal hex side length Bside to cover at least B sites."""
    if B <= 1:
        return 0
    s = 0
    while True:
        n = 1 + 3*s*(s+1)
        if n >= B:
            return s
        s += 1

def user_points(N=400, extent=1.0, traffic_type='uniform',
                hotspot_center=(0.25, 0.25), hotspot_radius=0.3, hotspot_ratio=0.6):
    if traffic_type == 'hotspot':
        Nh = int(N * hotspot_ratio)
        Nu = N - Nh
        theta = np.random.uniform(0, 2*np.pi, Nh)
        r = hotspot_radius * np.sqrt(np.random.uniform(0, 1, Nh))
        hx = hotspot_center[0] + r*np.cos(theta)
        hy = hotspot_center[1] + r*np.sin(theta)
        hu = np.stack([hx, hy], axis=1)
        uu = np.random.uniform(-extent, extent, size=(Nu, 2))
        return np.vstack([hu, uu])
    else:
        return np.random.uniform(-extent, extent, size=(N, 2))

# ------------------ Channel and Gains ------------------

def vertical_pattern(delta_deg, th3dB=8.0, Amax=25.0):
    return -np.minimum(12.0*(delta_deg/th3dB)**2, Amax)

def get_link_gains(bs_xy, ue_xy, M_sel, T_sel, h_bs=30.0, h_ue=1.5):
    """
    3GPP-like UMa large-scale pathloss and vertical antenna pattern; returns linear gains.
    """
    B, K = bs_xy.shape[0], ue_xy.shape[0]
    G = np.zeros((B, K), dtype=float)
    for b in range(B):
        for k in range(K):
            d2 = np.linalg.norm(bs_xy[b] - ue_xy[k])*1000.0 + 1.0
            d3 = np.sqrt(d2**2 + (h_bs - h_ue)**2)
            pl_db = 128.1 + 37.6*np.log10(d3/1000.0 + 1e-3)  # 3GPP UMa
            elev_deg = np.rad2deg(np.arctan2(h_bs - h_ue, d2))
            av_db = vertical_pattern(elev_deg - T_sel[b])
            G[b, k] = M_sel[b] * db2lin(-pl_db + av_db)
    return G

# ------------------ QUBO Construction and SA ------------------

def build_qubo(B, M_choices, T_choices, R_choices, bs_xy, ue_xy,
               lam_onehot=10.0, alpha=1.0, beta=0.05, gamma=1.0, cell_independent=False):
    """
    Construct QUBO matrix using baseline-delta inclusion-exclusion.
    Diagonal: local utility; Off-diagonal: inter-cell couplings (skipped if cell_independent=True).
    """
    M_list, T_list, R_list = list(M_choices), list(T_choices), list(R_choices)
    n_m, n_t, n_r = len(M_list), len(T_list), len(R_list)
    n_vars_per_b = n_m + n_t + n_r
    n_total_vars = B * n_vars_per_b

    def idx_map(b, p_type, p_idx):
        if p_type == 'M':
            offset = 0
        elif p_type == 'T':
            offset = n_m
        else:
            offset = n_m + n_t
        return b * n_vars_per_b + offset + p_idx

    Q = np.zeros((n_total_vars, n_total_vars), dtype=float)

    # Precompute large-scale gain cache for all (M, T) pairs for utility approximation
    gain_cache = {}
    for mi, mval in enumerate(M_list):
        for ti, tval in enumerate(T_list):
            M_tmp = np.full(B, mval, dtype=float)
            T_tmp = np.full(B, tval, dtype=float)
            gain_cache[(mi, ti)] = get_link_gains(bs_xy, ue_xy, M_tmp, T_tmp)

    # Baseline mid-range
    base_mi = min(1, n_m-1)
    base_ti = min(1, n_t-1)
    noise_W = db2lin(-174 + 10*np.log10(20e6))
    sinr_thresh = db2lin(5.0)

    # Assign users to Voronoi-like serving cell based on baseline (mid) config
    G_base_full = gain_cache[(base_mi, base_ti)]
    serving_bs = np.argmax(G_base_full, axis=0)

    # Diagonal terms: local contributions for M and T (R treated as overhead proxy)
    for b in range(B):
        ue_mask = (serving_bs == b)
        if not np.any(ue_mask):
            continue

        # M diagonal: energy and capacity deltas
        for mi, mval in enumerate(M_list):
            im = idx_map(b, 'M', mi)
            G_new = gain_cache[(mi, base_ti)][b, ue_mask]
            G_basel = gain_cache[(base_mi, base_ti)][b, ue_mask]
            cap_delta = np.sum(np.log2(1 + G_new / noise_W) - np.log2(1 + G_basel / noise_W))
            energy_delta = (mval - M_list[base_mi]) * 3.0  # 3 W per RF chain proxy
            util = alpha * cap_delta - beta * energy_delta
            Q[im, im] -= util

        # T diagonal: capacity and coverage deltas
        for ti, tval in enumerate(T_list):
            it = idx_map(b, 'T', ti)
            G_new = gain_cache[(base_mi, ti)][b, ue_mask]
            G_basel = gain_cache[(base_mi, base_ti)][b, ue_mask]
            cap_delta = np.sum(np.log2(1 + G_new / noise_W) - np.log2(1 + G_basel / noise_W))
            cov_delta = np.sum((G_new / noise_W) < sinr_thresh) - np.sum((G_basel / noise_W) < sinr_thresh)
            util = alpha * cap_delta - gamma * cov_delta
            Q[it, it] -= util

        # R diagonal: small overhead/capacity proxy (optional, simplified)
        for ri, rval in enumerate(R_list):
            ir = idx_map(b, 'R', ri)
            overhead_penalty = -alpha * 0.05 * (rval - 1)  # simple pilot overhead model
            Q[ir, ir] += overhead_penalty

    # Off-diagonal: inter-cell couplings (only if coordinated)
    if not cell_independent:
        for b in range(B):
            for bp in range(b + 1, B):
                ue_mask_victim = (serving_bs == bp)
                if not np.any(ue_mask_victim):
                    continue
                for mi, mval in enumerate(M_list):
                    im_interferer = idx_map(b, 'M', mi)
                    for ti, tval in enumerate(T_list):
                        it_victim = idx_map(bp, 'T', ti)
                        # baseline interferer contribution
                        interf_base = gain_cache[(base_mi, base_ti)][b, ue_mask_victim]
                        interf_new = gain_cache[(mi, base_ti)][b, ue_mask_victim]
                        sig_victim = gain_cache[(base_mi, ti)][bp, ue_mask_victim]
                        sinr_base = sig_victim / (noise_W + interf_base)
                        sinr_new = sig_victim / (noise_W + interf_new)
                        delta_util = alpha * np.sum(np.log2(1 + sinr_new) - np.log2(1 + sinr_base))
                        # negative of utility change because we minimize x^T Q x
                        Q[im_interferer, it_victim] -= delta_util

    # One-hot penalties per parameter per cell
    for b in range(B):
        for ptype, plist in [('M', M_list), ('T', T_list), ('R', R_list)]:
            indices = [idx_map(b, ptype, i) for i in range(len(plist))]
            for i in indices:
                Q[i, i] -= lam_onehot
            for i in range(len(indices)):
                for j in range(i + 1, len(indices)):
                    Q[indices[i], indices[j]] += 2 * lam_onehot

    return Q

def simulated_annealing_qubo(Q, MTR_lists, B, steps=30000, T0=1.0, T1=1e-4):
    """
    Simulated annealing on QUBO with one-hot neighborhood moves.
    """
    M_list, T_list, R_list = MTR_lists
    n_m, n_t, n_r = len(M_list), len(T_list), len(R_list)
    n_per_b = n_m + n_t + n_r

    def idxs(b, mi, ti, ri):
        return b*n_per_b + mi, b*n_per_b + n_m + ti, b*n_per_b + n_m + n_t + ri

    # Random valid initial solution
    x = np.zeros(Q.shape[0], dtype=int)
    m_sels = np.random.randint(0, n_m, B)
    t_sels = np.random.randint(0, n_t, B)
    r_sels = np.random.randint(0, n_r, B)
    for b in range(B):
        im, it, ir = idxs(b, m_sels[b], t_sels[b], r_sels[b])
        x[im] = x[it] = x[ir] = 1

    def energy(xvec):
        return float(xvec.T @ Q @ xvec)

    E = energy(x)
    best_x = x.copy()
    best_E = E

    for step in range(steps):
        T = T0 * (T1/T0)**(step/max(1, steps-1))
        b = np.random.randint(0, B)
        ptype = np.random.choice(['M', 'T', 'R'])
        x_new = x.copy()

        if ptype == 'M':
            old = m_sels[b]
            cand = (old + np.random.randint(1, n_m)) % n_m
            im_old, _, _ = idxs(b, old, t_sels[b], r_sels[b])
            im_new, _, _ = idxs(b, cand, t_sels[b], r_sels[b])
            x_new[im_old] = 0
            x_new[im_new] = 1
        elif ptype == 'T':
            old = t_sels[b]
            cand = (old + np.random.randint(1, n_t)) % n_t
            _, it_old, _ = idxs(b, m_sels[b], old, r_sels[b])
            _, it_new, _ = idxs(b, m_sels[b], cand, r_sels[b])
            x_new[it_old] = 0
            x_new[it_new] = 1
        else:
            old = r_sels[b]
            cand = (old + np.random.randint(1, n_r)) % n_r
            _, _, ir_old = idxs(b, m_sels[b], t_sels[b], old)
            _, _, ir_new = idxs(b, m_sels[b], t_sels[b], cand)
            x_new[ir_old] = 0
            x_new[ir_new] = 1

        E_new = energy(x_new)
        dE = E_new - E
        if dE < 0 or np.random.rand() < math.exp(-dE / max(T, 1e-12)):
            x = x_new
            E = E_new
            if ptype == 'M':
                m_sels[b] = cand
            elif ptype == 'T':
                t_sels[b] = cand
            else:
                r_sels[b] = cand
            if E < best_E:
                best_E = E
                best_x = x.copy()

    return best_x

def decode_solution(x, MTR_lists, B):
    M_list, T_list, R_list = MTR_lists
    n_m, n_t, n_r = len(M_list), len(T_list), len(R_list)
    n_per_b = n_m + n_t + n_r
    M_sel = np.zeros(B, dtype=int)
    T_sel = np.zeros(B, dtype=int)
    R_sel = np.zeros(B, dtype=int)
    for b in range(B):
        seg = x[b*n_per_b:(b+1)*n_per_b]
        mvec = seg[:n_m]
        tvec = seg[n_m:n_m+n_t]
        rvec = seg[n_m+n_t:]
        M_sel[b] = M_list[np.argmax(mvec)] if mvec.sum() > 0 else M_list[0]
        T_sel[b] = T_list[np.argmax(tvec)] if tvec.sum() > 0 else T_list[0]
        R_sel[b] = R_list[np.argmax(rvec)] if rvec.sum() > 0 else R_list[0]
    return M_sel, T_sel, R_sel

# ------------------ Evaluation and Baselines ------------------

def evaluate_solution(M_sel, T_sel, R_sel, bs_xy, ue_xy):
    """
    Returns (sum_rate_gbps, p5_mbps, power_W, energy_eff_Mbps_per_W).
    """
    B = len(M_sel)
    G = get_link_gains(bs_xy, ue_xy, M_sel, T_sel)
    noise_W = db2lin(-174 + 10*np.log10(20e6))
    serving_bs = np.argmax(G, axis=0)

    user_sinr = np.zeros(ue_xy.shape[0], dtype=float)
    for k in range(ue_xy.shape[0]):
        b = serving_bs[k]
        sig = G[b, k]
        interf = np.sum(G[:, k]) - sig
        # Pilot contamination proxy: add interference from cells in same reuse class as serving
        pilot_interf = 0.0
        if R_sel[b] > 1:
            for bp in range(B):
                if bp != b and (bp % R_sel[b]) == (b % R_sel[b]):
                    pilot_interf += G[bp, k]
        user_sinr[k] = sig / (noise_W + interf + pilot_interf)

    user_rates_Mbps = 20e6 * np.log2(1 + user_sinr) / 1e6
    sum_rate_gbps = float(np.sum(user_rates_Mbps) / 1000.0)
    p5_mbps = float(np.percentile(user_rates_Mbps, 5))
    power_W = float(np.sum(500.0 + 3.0*np.array(M_sel)))  # 500W static + 3W per RF chain
    energy_eff = float(np.sum(user_rates_Mbps) / power_W)
    return sum_rate_gbps, p5_mbps, power_W, energy_eff

def run_greedy_solver(bs_xy, ue_xy, M_choices, T_choices, R_choices):
    B = bs_xy.shape[0]
    M = np.random.choice(M_choices, B)
    T = np.random.choice(T_choices, B)
    R = np.random.choice(R_choices, B)

    for _ in range(3):  # 3 passes
        for b in range(B):
            best = (None, -1e9)
            for m in M_choices:
                for t in T_choices:
                    for r in R_choices:
                        M[b], T[b], R[b] = m, t, r
                        sr, p5, pw, ee = evaluate_solution(M, T, R, bs_xy, ue_xy)
                        util = sr - 0.005*pw  # proxy utility
                        if util > best[1]:
                            best = ((m, t, r), util)
            M[b], T[b], R[b] = best[0]
    return M, T, R

def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument('--B', type=int, default=7)
    ap.add_argument('--runs', type=int, default=20)
    ap.add_argument('--traffic', type=str, default='hotspot', choices=['uniform','hotspot'])
    ap.add_argument('--steps', type=int, default=30000)
    ap.add_argument('--users', type=int, default=400)
    ap.add_argument('--plot', action='store_true', help='Generate results_plot.pdf from coordinated QUBO-SA solution (first run)')
    args = ap.parse_args()

    M_choices = (32, 64, 128)
    T_choices = (6, 10, 14)
    R_choices = (1, 3)
    MTR_lists = (M_choices, T_choices, R_choices)

    methods = ['Static-Max', 'Greedy', 'QUBO-Indep', 'QUBO-Coordinated']
    results = {m: [] for m in methods}

    print(f'=== Simulation: B={args.B}, runs={args.runs}, traffic={args.traffic} ===')
    for run in range(args.runs):
        print(f'Run {run+1}/{args.runs}')
        seed_all(run)
        # Ensure we generate enough BS locations for requested B
        Bside_needed = min_Bside_for_B(args.B)
        bs_xy = hex_grid(Bside=Bside_needed, spacing_km=0.5)[:args.B]
        ue_xy = user_points(N=args.users, extent=1.0, traffic_type=args.traffic)

        # Static-Max
        M_sm = np.full(args.B, 128)
        T_sm = np.full(args.B, 10)
        R_sm = np.full(args.B, 1)
        results['Static-Max'].append(evaluate_solution(M_sm, T_sm, R_sm, bs_xy, ue_xy))

        # Greedy
        M_g, T_g, R_g = run_greedy_solver(bs_xy, ue_xy, M_choices, T_choices, R_choices)
        results['Greedy'].append(evaluate_solution(M_g, T_g, R_g, bs_xy, ue_xy))

        # QUBO Independent
        Q_indep = build_qubo(args.B, M_choices, T_choices, R_choices, bs_xy, ue_xy, cell_independent=True)
        x_indep = simulated_annealing_qubo(Q_indep, MTR_lists, args.B, steps=args.steps)
        M_i, T_i, R_i = decode_solution(x_indep, MTR_lists, args.B)
        results['QUBO-Indep'].append(evaluate_solution(M_i, T_i, R_i, bs_xy, ue_xy))

        # QUBO Coordinated
        Q_coord = build_qubo(args.B, M_choices, T_choices, R_choices, bs_xy, ue_xy, cell_independent=False)
        x_coord = simulated_annealing_qubo(Q_coord, MTR_lists, args.B, steps=args.steps)
        M_c, T_c, R_c = decode_solution(x_coord, MTR_lists, args.B)
        results['QUBO-Coordinated'].append(evaluate_solution(M_c, T_c, R_c, bs_xy, ue_xy))

        if args.plot and run == 0:
            plot_config(bs_xy, ue_xy, M_c, T_c, args.traffic)

    # Print summary
    print('\n--- Results Summary (mean +/- std) ---')
    for m in methods:
        arr = np.array(results[m])
        mean = arr.mean(axis=0)
        std = arr.std(axis=0)
        print(f'{m:>16}:  SR={mean[0]:.2f}+/-{std[0]:.2f}  P5={mean[1]:.2f}+/-{std[1]:.2f}  P={mean[2]:.1f}+/-{std[2]:.1f}  EE={mean[3]:.2f}+/-{std[3]:.2f}')

if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
