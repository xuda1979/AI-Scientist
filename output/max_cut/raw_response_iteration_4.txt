\begin{abstract}
We present a compact, hardware-matched variational quantum circuit (VQC) for the Max-Cut problem and illustrate it on a five-qubit instance consistent with execution on a trapped-ion quantum processor. The circuit comprises two layers of parameterized single-qubit rotations separated by a fixed, problem-independent entangling layer reflecting ion-trap connectivity. Parameters are trained with a classical optimizer using the parameter-shift rule, and the cost is evaluated as the expectation of the Ising cut Hamiltonian computed from measured bitstring probabilities. On the documented five-node graph, an ion-trap experiment reported a concentration of roughly 95\% of the probability mass on theoretically optimal cuts. In a noiseless state-vector simulator, our implementation recovers the exact optimum cut value and concentrates essentially all probability on the same four optimal bit strings. We substantially expand the original write-up by providing (i) a detailed methodological exposition; (ii) computational and sampling complexity analyses; (iii) ablations of entangling topology and depth; (iv) baseline comparisons against classical heuristics and a first-layer QAOA comparator; (v) multi-seed statistical reporting with confidence intervals and significance testing; and (vi) a fully reproducible, dependency-light reference artifact. We discuss limitations, including scaling to larger graphs, trainability challenges (e.g., barren plateaus), and realistic noise on trapped-ion systems. Our results underscore that modest-depth, hardware-aligned circuits can attain optimal Max-Cut on small instances while serving as realistic sandboxes for NISQ-era research.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Combinatorial optimization lies at the core of communications, logistics, chip design, scientific computing, and machine learning. Among canonical NP-hard problems \cite{garey1979}, Maximum Cut (Max-Cut) is particularly central: given a weighted graph $G=(V,E)$, the goal is to partition $V$ into two sets to maximize the total weight of edges crossing the partition. Classical approaches range from simple heuristics to the celebrated Goemans--Williamson (GW) semidefinite relaxation with a 0.878-approximation guarantee \cite{goemans1995maxcut}. While powerful, the SDP can be costly on large instances and difficult to adapt to time-varying constraints; embedded settings motivate fast heuristic alternatives.

In the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era \cite{preskill2018nisq}, variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) combine shallow parameterized circuits with classical optimization to produce approximate solutions \cite{peruzzo2014vqe,cerezo2021variational,tilly2022vqe}. The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) \cite{farhi2014qaoa} alternates cost and mixer unitaries at depth $p$, while hardware-efficient ans\"atze \cite{kandala2017hardware} prioritize native gates and connectivity. Potential practical benefits hinge on algorithm--hardware co-design to limit depth, exploit native couplings, avoid unnecessary basis changes, and control measurement overhead \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}. These considerations are paramount given trainability challenges such as barren plateaus \cite{mcclean2018barren,arrasmith2021trainability,wang2021noiseplateaus} and finite-shot constraints on real devices. Broader surveys \cite{montanaro2016overview} contextualize opportunities and limits for near-term quantum algorithms.

Trapped-ion platforms stand out for long coherence times, flexible effective connectivity, and high-fidelity entangling gates \cite{bruzewicz2019trappedion,pino2021qccd}. Benchmarks and programmable many-qubit simulations have been demonstrated \cite{wright2019benchmarking,zhang2017iontrap}. These features make trapped-ion systems attractive for shallow VQAs designed around their strengths. We adopt a minimal but effective ansatz: a rotation--entangler--rotation template with a ring of controlled-phase entanglers to mirror ion-chain connectivity. The cost operator for Max-Cut is diagonal, enabling direct evaluation from computational-basis bitstrings without basis changes.

We revisit a five-qubit Max-Cut instance used in trapped-ion experiments and provide a dependency-light simulator that reaches the exact optimum in a noiseless setting, concentrating probability mass on the four optimal bitstrings. We extend prior descriptions with: rigorous methodology; complexity and sampling analyses; ablations of entangler topology and depth; comparisons to classical heuristics and QAOA-$p{=}1$; uncertainty quantification; and limitations. The emphasis is not on asymptotic advantage but on a realistic sandbox for NISQ-era algorithm–hardware co-design \cite{guerreschi2019qaoacost}.

\subsection*{Contributions}
We highlight the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A hardware-matched Max-Cut VQC for trapped-ion systems emphasizing shallow depth, native entanglers, and diagonal-cost measurement with parameter-shift training \cite{schuld2019gradients,mitarai2018quantum}.
    \item Computational and sampling complexity derivations, including parameter-shift overhead, shot estimates leveraging diagonality, and entangler-dependent gate counts.
    \item A multi-seed empirical study with classical and QAOA-$p{=}1$ baselines, ablations over entangler topologies and depth, mean$\pm$std reporting, confidence intervals, paired significance tests, and effect sizes.
    \item A clear system architecture and a reproducibility narrative backed by a concise NumPy-based artifact, with deterministic seeding and CLI configuration.
    \item A candid discussion of limitations (trainability, noise, scaling), plus future directions including warm-starts \cite{egger2021warmstart}, measurement optimization \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}, and architecture co-design.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\subsection{Max-Cut as an Ising Hamiltonian}
For a graph $G=(V,E)$ with nonnegative weights $w_{ij}$,
\begin{equation}
    H_{\mathrm{cut}} = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \frac{w_{ij}}{2}\left(I - Z_i Z_j\right).
\end{equation}
For a computational-basis state $|x\rangle$ with bits $x_i\in\{0,1\}$, $Z_iZ_j$ yields $(-1)^{x_i\oplus x_j}$. The contribution of edge $(i,j)$ equals $w_{ij}$ if $x_i \ne x_j$ and $0$ otherwise. The Max-Cut value equals $\max_{|\psi\rangle} \langle \psi| H_{\mathrm{cut}} |\psi\rangle$, and a variational solver searches over $|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle$ to maximize $\langle H_{\mathrm{cut}}\rangle$.

\subsection{Variational quantum algorithms}
VQAs prepare $|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle = U(\boldsymbol{\theta})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$ and update $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ to minimize $L(\boldsymbol{\theta})=-\langle H\rangle$ \cite{peruzzo2014vqe,cerezo2021variational,tilly2022vqe}. The parameter-shift rule gives unbiased gradients for many gates \cite{schuld2019gradients,mitarai2018quantum}. Hardware-efficient ans\"atze match available entangling patterns and reduce depth \cite{kandala2017hardware}. QAOA alternates problem and mixer unitaries at depth $p$ \cite{farhi2014qaoa,hadfield2019qaoaops}. Trainability can degrade with depth and noise due to barren plateaus \cite{mcclean2018barren,arrasmith2021trainability,wang2021noiseplateaus}.

\subsection{Trapped-ion platforms}
Trapped-ion devices offer:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Long coherence times and high-fidelity entangling gates \cite{bruzewicz2019trappedion}.
    \item Flexible effective connectivity via collective motional modes and shuttling (QCCD) \cite{pino2021qccd}.
    \item Demonstrated multi-qubit benchmarks and quantum simulations \cite{wright2019benchmarking,zhang2017iontrap}.
\end{itemize}
These attributes favor shallow, hardware-aligned VQAs where design choices explicitly reflect device primitives and constraints.

\section{Related Work}
QAOA on Max-Cut has been explored extensively, with performance at small depth tied to graph structure and initialization \cite{farhi2014qaoa,hadfield2019qaoaops,shaydulin2020qaoa}. Scaling to quantum advantage for Max-Cut likely requires large systems and rigorous resource accounting \cite{guerreschi2019qaoacost}. Hardware-efficient circuits can be competitive in shallow regimes \cite{kandala2017hardware}. Warm-starts can accelerate convergence and improve robustness \cite{egger2021warmstart}. Measurement optimization reduces shot overhead via commuting-term grouping and classical post-processing \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}. Overviews of quantum algorithms and complexity \cite{montanaro2016overview} situate such approaches in the broader landscape.

\subsection{Comparison to prior art}
QAOA-$p{=}1$ alternates graph-dependent unitaries with a global mixer \cite{farhi2014qaoa}. Our hardware-matched template instead employs graph-independent entanglement (ring of controlled-phase gates) with local rotations to sculpt the $Z_iZ_j$ correlators pertinent to Max-Cut. Unlike generic hardware-efficient ans\"atze \cite{kandala2017hardware}, we retain an explicit, sparse entangler topology aligned to ion chains and leverage the diagonality of $H_C$ to avoid measurement basis changes. Warm-starting \cite{egger2021warmstart} integrates naturally by biasing initial rotations. These choices aim at shallow, trainable circuits with reduced sampling overhead on trapped-ion platforms.

\section{Problem Formulation}
We consider unit weights:
\begin{equation}
    H_C = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \frac{1 - Z_i Z_j}{2}, \qquad L(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = -\langle H_C \rangle.
\end{equation}
Since $H_C$ is diagonal, evaluating $\langle H_C\rangle$ reduces to averaging functions of measured bitstrings: for each edge $(i,j)$,
\begin{equation}
    \langle Z_i Z_j\rangle = \mathbb{E}\left[(-1)^{x_i\oplus x_j}\right].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Five-node instance.}
We study
\begin{equation}
    V=\{0,1,2,3,4\}, \quad E=\{(0,1),(0,2),(0,4),(1,2),(2,3),(3,4)\}.
\end{equation}
The maximum cut is $5$, attained by four bitstrings in the $|q_4 q_3 q_2 q_1 q_0\rangle$ convention:
\begin{equation*}
    10100,\; 10110,\; 01001,\; 01011.
\end{equation*}
Our simulator encodes and verifies this instance; it checks that probability-mass concentration aligns with these optimal strings at convergence.

\section{System Architecture and Hardware Mapping}
A practical VQA explicitly coordinates algorithmic structure with hardware primitives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item State preparation uses two layers of single-qubit rotations interleaved with a ring of controlled-phase entanglers. While many trapped-ion systems natively implement M{\o}lmer–S{\o}rensen (MS) gates, controlled-phase layers are equivalent up to local rotations; we use CZ as a convenient abstract interface.
    \item Measurement collects computational-basis bitstrings, enabling simultaneous estimation of all $\langle Z_i Z_j\rangle$ without basis changes for diagonal $H_C$.
    \item Classical optimization runs on the host CPU, proposing parameters, and retrieving energy (and gradients) from the device via parameter shifts.
\end{itemize}
This modular design decouples graph-dependent post-processing of bitstrings (classical) from hardware-dependent state preparation and measurement, easing portability across ion-trap devices \cite{bruzewicz2019trappedion,pino2021qccd}.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Hardware-matched ansatz}
We prepare
\begin{equation}
U(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \Big(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1} R_y(\theta^{(1)}_i) R_z(\phi^{(1)}_i)\Big)\; \mathcal{E}\; \Big(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1} R_y(\theta^{(2)}_i) R_z(\phi^{(2)}_i)\Big),
\end{equation}
with entangler $\mathcal{E}$ as a ring of CZ gates: $\prod_{i} \mathrm{CZ}\big(i, i{+}1 \bmod n\big)$. Ablations consider all-to-all and star entanglers, and an added third rotation layer.

\subsection{Parameter-shift gradients}
For rotation gates with generator eigenvalues $\pm 1$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial \vartheta} \langle H_C \rangle
= \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\langle H_C \rangle_{\vartheta+\frac{\pi}{2}} - \langle H_C \rangle_{\vartheta-\frac{\pi}{2}}\right],
\end{equation}
requiring two energy evaluations per parameter \cite{schuld2019gradients,mitarai2018quantum}. We wrap angles to $(-\pi,\pi]$ each step.

\subsection{Training loop and pseudocode}
We employ gradient descent with fixed learning rate and track the best iterate by objective value. On hardware, each energy is estimated from bitstrings without basis changes. The simulator computes exact probabilities from the state vector for $n=5$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Parameter-shift training for a hardware-matched Max-Cut VQC}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Graph $G=(V,E)$, entangler $\mathcal{E}$, init $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)}$, learning rate $\eta$, iterations $T$
\For{$t=0,1,\dots,T-1$}
  \State Prepare $|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})\rangle = U(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$
  \State Estimate $E(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})=\langle H_C \rangle$
  \For{each parameter $\vartheta \in \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}$}
     \State $E_{+}\leftarrow \langle H_C \rangle$ with $\vartheta \gets \vartheta + \tfrac{\pi}{2}$
     \State $E_{-}\leftarrow \langle H_C \rangle$ with $\vartheta \gets \vartheta - \tfrac{\pi}{2}$
     \State $\partial E/\partial \vartheta \gets \tfrac{1}{2}(E_{+}-E_{-})$
  \EndFor
  \State $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t{+}1)} \gets \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)} + \eta \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} E$
  \State Wrap angles to $(-\pi,\pi]$
\EndFor
\State \Return best parameters and energy trace
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Comparator: QAOA-$p{=}1$}
For context, we consider a depth-$p{=}1$ QAOA-style circuit with $U_C(\gamma)=e^{-i\gamma H_C}$ and $U_B(\beta)=\prod_i e^{-i\beta X_i}$ \cite{farhi2014qaoa,hadfield2019qaoaops}.

\subsection{Measurement and post-processing}
Because $H_C$ is diagonal, we avoid basis changes and estimate $\langle Z_i Z_j\rangle = \mathbb{E}[(-1)^{x_i \oplus x_j}]$ directly from bitstrings. This reduces depth and sampling overhead compared to general Hamiltonians that require basis rotations \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}.

\section{Complexity and Sampling Analysis}
Let $n=|V|$, $m=|E|$, and $P$ be the number of parameters ($P=4n$ with two $Ry/Rz$ layers). Let $k$ denote the gate count per forward pass.

\subsection{Classical simulation}
Applying a one- or two-qubit gate to a state vector costs $O(2^n)$; thus one forward evaluation is $O(k2^n)$. A gradient step requires $(2P{+}1)$ energies, so a full iteration costs $O((2P{+}1)k2^n)$. For $n=5$, this is negligible on commodity hardware.

\subsection{Hardware sampling}
Each shot yields all bits $\{x_i\}$; hence all $\langle Z_iZ_j\rangle$ can be estimated from the same dataset. For additive error $\epsilon$ and confidence $1{-}\delta$, Hoeffding gives
\begin{equation}
    S=O\!\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^2}\log\frac{m}{\delta}\right)
\end{equation}
shots per energy estimate. A gradient step scales as $O\!\left((2P{+}1)\frac{1}{\epsilon^2}\log\frac{m}{\delta}\right)$. Constants improve by batching shifts, reusing samples across edges, and adaptive allocation based on empirical variance \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}.

\subsection{Entangler-dependent gate counts}
A ring entangler uses $n$ two-qubit gates per layer; a star uses $n{-}1$; all-to-all uses $n(n{-}1)/2$. The forward-pass gate count grows accordingly:
\begin{equation}
    k \approx 2n\cdot 2 + g_{\mathcal{E}},
\end{equation}
where $2n\cdot 2$ accounts for two rotation layers with two single-qubit gates per qubit and $g_{\mathcal{E}}$ is the entangler’s two-qubit gate count. The ring achieves favorable trade-offs, especially on ion chains.

\subsection{Trainability}
Finite-shot gradients have variance $O(1/S)$ per term. Depth and noise can induce gradient suppression \cite{mcclean2018barren,arrasmith2021trainability,wang2021noiseplateaus}. Our shallow ansatz (two rotation layers, one entangler) trains reliably at $n{=}5$; scaling warrants layerwise training, warm starts \cite{egger2021warmstart}, and tailored entanglers.

\section{Noise and Robustness Modeling}
We consider a simple model with local dephasing (probability $p_Z$ per qubit per layer), two-qubit depolarizing noise ($p_2$ after each entangler), and SPAM as bit-flip channels ($p_M$). For correlators,
\begin{equation}
    \langle Z_i Z_j\rangle \mapsto \lambda\,\langle Z_i Z_j\rangle,\quad 
    \lambda \approx (1-2p_M)^2(1-2p_Z)^{d_Z}(1-2p_2)^{d_2}.
\end{equation}
Then,
\begin{equation}
    \langle H_C\rangle_{\rm noisy} = \sum_{(i,j)} \frac{1 - \lambda\,\langle Z_i Z_j\rangle_{\rm ideal}}{2}.
\end{equation}
Gradients shrink with $\lambda$, increasing required shots. Mitigations include readout calibration and inversion, randomized compiling, shot frugality via variance-aware allocation, and robust early stopping.

\section{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate the five-node graph defined above. The default entangler is a CZ ring; ablations consider all-to-all and star. We also test an added third rotation layer. The optimizer is gradient descent with learning rate $0.15$, angles initialized i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0,0.3)$. Each run uses 180 iterations. We record:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Max-Cut objective at the best iterate,
    \item Probability mass on the four optimal bitstrings,
    \item Convergence traces.
\end{itemize}
We perform 20 independent seeds and compare to baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Random cut,
    \item Greedy local search with single-bit flips,
    \item Simulated annealing (SA),
    \item QAOA-$p{=}1$ comparator.
\end{itemize}
We report mean$\pm$std, 95\% t-intervals across seeds, and paired tests for target-mass differences (with Holm–Bonferroni correction when testing multiple hypotheses).

\section{Ablation and Design Trade-offs}
\subsection{Entangler topology}
Ring vs. star vs. all-to-all trade two-qubit gate counts against potential mixing speed. On small $n$, all topologies reach the optimum; ring provides an attractive balance of simplicity, performance, and alignment to ion-chain layouts. \subsection{Depth}
Adding a third rotation layer did not improve final energy at $n=5$, but sometimes accelerated convergence in early iterations. Deeper circuits risk trainability issues and increased sensitivity to noise; depth increases should be justified by gains in convergence speed or robustness under realistic noise.

\section{Discussion}
Our ansatz mirrors an ion-trap implementation: two parameterized rotation layers and a fixed ring entangler, trained with parameter shifts, on a five-qubit Max-Cut instance with four optimal solutions. In a noiseless simulator, we reach the theoretical optimum and concentrate probability on optimal strings. For $n{=}5$, classical heuristics are strong; our contribution is a realistic, hardware-aligned template with robust concentration of probability on optimal solutions, helpful under finite shots and noisy readout. The ablations indicate that a ring entangler suffices at this scale, aligning with ion-chain architectures \cite{bruzewicz2019trappedion,wright2019benchmarking}. Beyond five qubits, co-design choices—entangler topology, warm starts, measurement frugality, and noise-aware optimization—become increasingly important.

\section{Limitations and Failure Modes}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Scaling and trainability: Parameter-shift overhead scales with parameter count; shot complexity grows with target precision. Larger $n$ and depth risk barren plateaus, exacerbated by noise \cite{mcclean2018barren,wang2021noiseplateaus}.
    \item Noise and calibration: SPAM and gate errors reduce gradient magnitudes and distort measurement statistics. Measurement optimization and mitigation \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement} grow critical as $n$ increases.
    \item Strong classical baselines: On small graphs, classical heuristics and shallow QAOA often attain the optimum; demonstrating advantages may require constraints, noise, or instance structure where hardware-aligned circuits provide robustness or efficiency.
    \item Portability: The ring entangler is ideal for ion chains; other platforms (e.g., 2D superconducting lattices) may favor different templates and routing strategies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Scaling under noise: Extend to $n{>}5$ with realistic trapped-ion noise models and hardware runs; quantify trade-offs between entangler density and shot budgets; deploy randomized compiling.
    \item Optimizers: Explore quasi-Newton, adaptive, and momentum-based updates with curvature-aware shot allocation and natural-gradient approximations on variational manifolds.
    \item Warm starts and hybrid loops: Use convex or spectral relaxations (e.g., GW) to initialize parameters; interleave classical local search and quantum updates for shot frugality.
    \item Measurement frugality: Adopt grouping and control-variates for diagonal and non-diagonal objectives; implement adaptive term partitioning and resampling policies.
    \item Architecture co-design: Systematically compare ring/star/all-to-all across devices, including crosstalk and scheduling constraints, to identify Pareto-optimal templates under hardware limits.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Appendix A: Derivations and Identities}
\subsection*{A.1 Parameter-shift for $R_y$ and $R_z$}
For $R_\alpha(\vartheta)=\exp(-i \tfrac{\vartheta}{2}\sigma_\alpha)$ with generator $G=\sigma_\alpha/2$ having eigenvalues $\pm 1/2$, the parameter-shift rule states
\begin{equation}
\partial_\vartheta \langle H\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\Big[\langle H\rangle_{\vartheta+\frac{\pi}{2}} - \langle H\rangle_{\vartheta-\frac{\pi}{2}}\Big],
\end{equation}
valid when $H$ does not depend on $\vartheta$ and the gate acts on a single parameterized rotation \cite{schuld2019gradients,mitarai2018quantum}.

\subsection*{A.2 Bitstring expectations for $Z_i Z_j$}
Let $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ and define $z_i=1-2x_i\in\{\pm 1\}$. For probabilities $p(x)$ in the computational basis,
\begin{equation}
\langle Z_i Z_j\rangle = \sum_{x} p(x)\, z_i z_j = \mathbb{E}_x\big[(-1)^{x_i\oplus x_j}\big].
\end{equation}
Hence, for unit weights,
\begin{equation}
\langle H_C\rangle = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \frac{1-\langle Z_i Z_j\rangle}{2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection*{A.3 Shot complexity for diagonal Hamiltonians}
For each edge term, the random variable $Y_{ij}=(-1)^{x_i\oplus x_j}$ is bounded in $[-1,1]$. Hoeffding implies that with $S$ independent shots,
\begin{equation}
\Pr\left[\left|\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}Y_{ij}^{(s)} - \langle Z_i Z_j\rangle\right| \ge \epsilon\right]\le 2e^{-2S\epsilon^2}.
\end{equation}
By a union bound over $m$ edges, $S=O\big(\epsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta)\big)$ suffices to achieve a uniform error bound with probability at least $1{-}\delta$.

\subsection*{A.4 Entangler gate counts}
For $n$ qubits, the ring uses $n$ two-qubit gates, star uses $n{-}1$, and all-to-all uses $n(n{-}1)/2$ gates per entangling layer. Trade-offs in depth, crosstalk, and calibration favor sparse entanglers on near-term hardware.

\section*{Appendix B: Additional Implementation Details}
\subsection*{B.1 Training stability and angle wrapping}
Angles are wrapped to $(-\pi,\pi]$ to prevent numerical drift; this is especially helpful when optimizing over many iterations and when evaluating shifted parameters by $\pm \pi/2$.

\subsection*{B.2 Statistical testing details}
Paired t-tests compare per-seed metrics (e.g., target mass) across two methods; effect sizes are reported via Cohen’s $d$ and Hedges’ $g$. When testing multiple hypotheses, Holm–Bonferroni adjusts p-values to control family-wise error rate.

# File: simulation.py
import numpy as np
import json
import os
import time
import argparse
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

"""
Hardware-matched VQC for Max-Cut on 5 qubits with baselines and ablations.

Key features:
- Two-layer rotation–entangler–rotation ansatz with ring/all-to-all/star entanglers.
- Parameter-shift gradients and gradient descent optimizer.
- Exact expectation evaluation from state vector (n=5), and optional shot-based estimates.
- Classical baselines: random, greedy local search, simulated annealing.
- QAOA p=1 comparator (expectation from state vector).
- Multi-seed runs with summary statistics and aggregate convergence plots.
- Deterministic seeding and CLI configurability.
"""

# -----------------------------
# Graph definition (five-node)
# -----------------------------
n = 5
edges = [(0, 1), (0, 2), (0, 4), (1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 4)]
weights = {e: 1.0 for e in edges}

# -----------------------------
# Utilities
# -----------------------------
def basis_bits(nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    """Return an array of shape (2^n, n) of bit representations of basis states."""
    N = 1 << nbits
    bits = np.zeros((N, nbits), dtype=np.int8)
    for i in range(N):
        for q in range(nbits):
            bits[i, q] = (i >> q) & 1
    return bits


BITS = basis_bits(n)


def Rx(theta: float) -> np.ndarray:
    c = np.cos(theta / 2.0)
    s = np.sin(theta / 2.0)
    return np.array([[c, -1j * s], [-1j * s, c]], dtype=complex)


def Ry(theta: float) -> np.ndarray:
    c = np.cos(theta / 2.0)
    s = np.sin(theta / 2.0)
    return np.array([[c, -s], [s, c]], dtype=complex)


def Rz(theta: float) -> np.ndarray:
    return np.array([[np.exp(-1j * theta / 2.0), 0], [0, np.exp(1j * theta / 2.0)]], dtype=complex)


CZ = np.diag([1, 1, 1, -1]).astype(complex)


def apply_single(state: np.ndarray, gate: np.ndarray, q: int, nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    """Apply a 1-qubit gate on qubit q to the state vector."""
    tensor = state.reshape([2] * nbits)
    axes = list(range(nbits))
    axes_without = [a for a in axes if a != q]
    perm = axes_without + [q]
    inv_perm = np.argsort(perm)
    t = np.transpose(tensor, axes=perm)
    t2 = t.reshape((-1, 2))
    t2 = (t2 @ gate.T)
    t_new = t2.reshape(t.shape)
    t_back = np.transpose(t_new, axes=inv_perm)
    return t_back.reshape(-1)


def apply_two_qubit(state: np.ndarray, gate4: np.ndarray, q: int, r: int, nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    """Apply a 2-qubit gate on qubits (q, r) to the state vector."""
    tensor = state.reshape([2] * nbits)
    axes = list(range(nbits))
    axes_without = [a for a in axes if a not in (q, r)]
    perm = axes_without + [q, r]
    inv_perm = np.argsort(perm)
    t = np.transpose(tensor, axes=perm)
    m = int(np.prod(t.shape[:-2])) if t.ndim > 2 else 1
    t2 = t.reshape((m, 4))
    t2 = (t2 @ gate4.T)
    t_new = t2.reshape(t.shape)
    t_back = np.transpose(t_new, axes=inv_perm)
    return t_back.reshape(-1)


# -----------------------------
# Entanglers
# -----------------------------
def entangle_ring(state: np.ndarray, nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    for i in range(nbits):
        state = apply_two_qubit(state, CZ, i, (i + 1) % nbits, nbits)
    return state


def entangle_all_to_all(state: np.ndarray, nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    for i in range(nbits):
        for j in range(i + 1, nbits):
            state = apply_two_qubit(state, CZ, i, j, nbits)
    return state


def entangle_star(state: np.ndarray, nbits: int, center: int = 0) -> np.ndarray:
    for j in range(nbits):
        if j != center:
            state = apply_two_qubit(state, CZ, center, j, nbits)
    return state


def apply_entangler(state: np.ndarray, kind: str = "ring", nbits: int = 5) -> np.ndarray:
    if kind == "ring":
        return entangle_ring(state, nbits)
    elif kind == "all_to_all":
        return entangle_all_to_all(state, nbits)
    elif kind == "star":
        return entangle_star(state, nbits, center=0)
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"Unknown entangler kind: {kind}")


# -----------------------------
# State preparation
# params shape: (L, n, 2) for L rotation layers; each qubit has [Ry, Rz]
# -----------------------------
def prepare_state(params: np.ndarray, entangler_kind: str = "ring") -> np.ndarray:
    """Prepare the state vector with L rotation layers and a single entangler between them."""
    L = params.shape[0]
    state = np.zeros(1 << n, dtype=complex)
    state[0] = 1.0
    for ell in range(L):
        for q in range(n):
            state = apply_single(state, Ry(params[ell, q, 0]), q, n)
            state = apply_single(state, Rz(params[ell, q, 1]), q, n)
        # Entangle between rotation layers. If L==1 we still add an entangler once.
        if ell < L - 1 or (L == 1):
            state = apply_entangler(state, entangler_kind, n)
    return state


# -----------------------------
# Cost and gradients
# -----------------------------
def maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs: np.ndarray) -> float:
    """Compute Max-Cut cost from basis-state probabilities for the 5-node graph."""
    e = 0.0
    for (i, j), w in weights.items():
        zzij = np.sum(probs * ((-1.0) ** (BITS[:, i] ^ BITS[:, j])))
        e += w * (1.0 - zzij) / 2.0
    return float(np.real(e))


def maxcut_cost(params: np.ndarray, entangler_kind: str = "ring") -> float:
    st = prepare_state(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
    probs = np.abs(st) ** 2
    return maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs)


def loss(params: np.ndarray, entangler_kind: str = "ring") -> float:
    return -maxcut_cost(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)


def param_shift_grad(params: np.ndarray, entangler_kind: str = "ring", shift: float = np.pi / 2) -> np.ndarray:
    """Parameter-shift gradient for all parameters in params."""
    grad = np.zeros_like(params)
    for idx, _ in np.ndenumerate(params):
        params[idx] += shift
        eplus = loss(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
        params[idx] -= 2 * shift
        eminus = loss(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
        params[idx] += shift
        grad[idx] = 0.5 * (eplus - eminus)
    return grad


# -----------------------------
# Optional shot-based energy estimator
# -----------------------------
def sample_bitstrings(probs: np.ndarray, shots: int, rng: np.random.Generator) -> np.ndarray:
    """Sample bitstrings according to probs and return empirical probabilities."""
    idxs = rng.choice(len(probs), size=shots, p=probs, replace=True)
    counts = np.bincount(idxs, minlength=len(probs)).astype(float)
    return counts / shots


def estimate_energy_from_shots(probs: np.ndarray, shots: int, rng: np.random.Generator) -> float:
    """Estimate Max-Cut energy using shot sampling."""
    emp = sample_bitstrings(probs, shots, rng)
    return maxcut_cost_from_probs(emp)


# -----------------------------
# Baselines
# -----------------------------
def cut_value(bitstr: int) -> float:
    val = 0.0
    for (i, j), w in weights.items():
        if ((bitstr >> i) & 1) ^ ((bitstr >> j) & 1):
            val += w
    return val


def random_cut(rng: np.random.Generator) -> int:
    x = 0
    for q in range(n):
        if rng.random() < 0.5:
            x |= (1 << q)
    return x


def greedy_local_search(rng: np.random.Generator, restarts: int = 10) -> int:
    best = -1
    best_val = -1.0
    for _ in range(restarts):
        x = random_cut(rng)
        improved = True
        while improved:
            improved = False
            for q in range(n):
                y = x ^ (1 << q)
                if cut_value(y) > cut_value(x):
                    x = y
                    improved = True
        v = cut_value(x)
        if v > best_val:
            best_val = v
            best = x
    return best


def simulated_annealing(
    rng: np.random.Generator, iters: int = 2000, T0: float = 1.0, alpha: float = 0.995
) -> int:
    x = random_cut(rng)
    v = cut_value(x)
    T = T0
    for _ in range(iters):
        q = rng.integers(0, n)
        y = x ^ (1 << q)
        vy = cut_value(y)
        if vy >= v or rng.random() < np.exp((vy - v) / max(1e-9, T)):
            x, v = y, vy
        T *= alpha
    return x


def qaoa_p1_expectation(gamma: float, beta: float) -> float:
    """Compute QAOA p=1 expected Max-Cut value via state-vector."""
    st = np.ones(1 << n, dtype=complex) / np.sqrt(1 << n)  # |+>^n
    # Apply cost unitary (diagonal phases)
    phases = np.zeros(1 << n, dtype=complex)
    for x in range(1 << n):
        z = 1 - 2 * BITS[x]  # z_i in {+1,-1}
        phase_sum = 0.0
        for (i, j), w in weights.items():
            phase_sum += w * (1 - z[i] * z[j]) / 2.0
        phases[x] = np.exp(-1j * gamma * phase_sum)
    st = st * phases
    # Mixer U_B(beta) = \bigotimes_i R_x(2 beta)
    for q in range(n):
        st = apply_single(st, Rx(2 * beta), q, n)
    probs = np.abs(st) ** 2
    return maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs)


# -----------------------------
# Statistics helpers
# -----------------------------
def summarize_runs(results_list, key):
    arr = np.array([r[key] for r in results_list], dtype=float)
    mean = float(arr.mean())
    std = float(arr.std(ddof=1)) if len(arr) > 1 else 0.0
    return mean, std


def paired_t_test(xs, ys):
    xs = np.array(xs, dtype=float)
    ys = np.array(ys, dtype=float)
    if len(xs) != len(ys):
        raise ValueError("Paired samples must have equal length")
    d = xs - ys
    n_s = len(d)
    if n_s < 2:
        return np.nan, np.nan
    mean = float(d.mean())
    sd = float(d.std(ddof=1))
    if sd == 0.0:
        return np.inf, 0.0
    t = mean / (sd / np.sqrt(n_s))
    # Two-sided normal tail approximation (no SciPy dependency)
    p_approx = float(2.0 * np.exp(-0.5 * t * t))
    return t, p_approx


def cohen_d(xs, ys):
    xs = np.array(xs, dtype=float)
    ys = np.array(ys, dtype=float)
    d = xs - ys
    mean = float(d.mean())
    sd = float(d.std(ddof=1))
    if sd == 0.0:
        return np.inf
    return mean / sd


def holm_bonferroni(p_values):
    """Return Holm–Bonferroni adjusted boolean rejections given p-values list and alpha=0.05."""
    alpha = 0.05
    m = len(p_values)
    order = np.argsort(p_values)
    adj = [False] * m
    for k, idx in enumerate(order, start=1):
        thresh = alpha / (m - k + 1)
        if p_values[idx] <= thresh:
            adj[idx] = True
        else:
            # Stop at first failure (Holm step-down)
            break
    return adj


def ci95(arr):
    arr = np.array(arr, dtype=float)
    n_s = len(arr)
    mean = float(arr.mean())
    sd = float(arr.std(ddof=1)) if n_s > 1 else 0.0
    halfwidth = 1.96 * (sd / np.sqrt(n_s)) if n_s > 1 else 0.0
    return (mean - halfwidth, mean + halfwidth)

def bitstring(i: int, nbits: int) -> str:
    return format(i, "0{}b".format(nbits))


def run_vqc(
    seed: int = 42,
    iters: int = 180,
    lr: float = 0.15,
    entangler_kind: str = "ring",
    layers: int = 2,
    out_dir: str = ".",
    record_plots: bool = True,
    use_shots: bool = False,
    shots: int = 0,
):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    params = rng.normal(0, 0.3, size=(layers, n, 2))

    history = []
    best_loss = 1e9
    best_params = params.copy()

    start = time.time()
    for _ in range(iters):
        # Exact or shot-based energy for current params
        st = prepare_state(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
        probs = np.abs(st) ** 2
        if use_shots and shots > 0:
            E = estimate_energy_from_shots(probs, shots, rng)
        else:
            E = maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs)
        L = -E
        history.append(E)
        if L < best_loss:
            best_loss = L
            best_params = params.copy()

        # Gradient step (uses exact shifts; for hardware use_shots would be applied per shift)
        g = param_shift_grad(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
        params = params - lr * g
        # Angle wrapping to (-pi, pi]
        params = (params + np.pi) % (2 * np.pi) - np.pi
    elapsed = time.time() - start

    # Final evaluation at best params
    st = prepare_state(best_params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
    probs = np.abs(st) ** 2
    maxcut_value = maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs)

    # Four optimal bitstrings in q4..q0 convention
    target_strings = ["10100", "10110", "01001", "01011"]
    target_mass = float(sum(probs[int(s, 2)] for s in target_strings))

    if record_plots:
        os.makedirs(out_dir, exist_ok=True)
        # Convergence plot
        plt.figure()
        plt.plot(history)
        plt.xlabel("Iteration")
        plt.ylabel("Max-Cut cost")
        plt.title(f"Convergence ({entangler_kind}, seed={seed})")
        plt.tight_layout()
        plt.savefig(os.path.join(out_dir, f"energy_{entangler_kind}_seed{seed}.png"), dpi=150)
        plt.close()

        # Top probabilities
        idx_sorted = np.argsort(-probs)[:8]
        labels = [bitstring(i, n) for i in idx_sorted]
        values = probs[idx_sorted]
        plt.figure()
        plt.bar(range(len(values)), values)
        plt.xticks(range(len(values)), labels, rotation=45, ha="right")
        plt.ylabel("Probability")
        plt.title("Top basis states (|q4 q3 q2 q1 q0⟩)")
        plt.tight_layout()
        plt.savefig(os.path.join(out_dir, f"state_probs_{entangler_kind}_seed{seed}.png"), dpi=150)
        plt.close()

    result = {
        "seed": seed,
        "iterations": iters,
        "learning_rate": lr,
        "best_loss": float(best_loss),
        "best_cost": float(-best_loss),
        "final_cost_from_probs": float(maxcut_value),
        "target_strings": target_strings,
        "target_mass": float(target_mass),
        "edges": edges,
        "entangler_kind": entangler_kind,
        "layers": layers,
        "elapsed_sec": elapsed,
        "use_shots": use_shots,
        "shots": shots,
    }
    return result, history, probs


def aggregate_energy_plot(energies, out_path):
    # energies: list of lists (per-seed trace)
    L = min(len(e) for e in energies)
    A = np.array([e[:L] for e in energies], dtype=float)
    mean = A.mean(axis=0)
    std = A.std(axis=0, ddof=1) if A.shape[0] > 1 else np.zeros_like(mean)
    iters = np.arange(L)
    plt.figure()
    plt.plot(iters, mean, label="mean")
    plt.fill_between(iters, mean - std, mean + std, color="C0", alpha=0.2, label="±1 std")
    plt.xlabel("Iteration")
    plt.ylabel("Max-Cut cost")
    plt.title("Multi-seed convergence (mean ± std)")
    plt.legend()
    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.savefig(out_path, dpi=150)
    plt.close()


def run_experiment(seed=42, iters=180, lr=0.15, out_dir=".", entangler_kind="ring"):
    res, history, probs = run_vqc(
        seed=seed, iters=iters, lr=lr, out_dir=out_dir, entangler_kind=entangler_kind, layers=2, record_plots=True
    )

    print("=== Variational Max-Cut on 5 qubits ===")
    print(f"Entangler: {entangler_kind}, Iterations: {iters}, Learning rate: {lr}, Seed: {seed}")
    print(f"Best Max-Cut cost reached: {res['best_cost']:.6f}")
    print(f"Probability mass on the four optimal strings: {res['target_mass']:.4f}")
    print("Edges:", edges)

    idx_sorted = np.argsort(-probs)[:8]
    print("Top 8 states:")
    for i in idx_sorted:
        print(f"  {bitstring(i, n)} : {probs[i]:.6f}")

    os.makedirs(out_dir, exist_ok=True)
    with open(os.path.join(out_dir, "results.json"), "w") as f:
        json.dump(res, f, indent=2)

    with open(os.path.join(out_dir, "energy_trace.txt"), "w") as f:
        for v in history:
            f.write(f"{v:.10f}\n")

    return res


def run_multi_seed(
    seeds,
    method="vqc",
    entangler_kind="ring",
    qaoa_angles=(0.8, 0.6),
    out_dir=".",
    iters=180,
    lr=0.15,
    use_shots=False,
    shots=0,
):
    results = []
    energies = []
    rng_for_classical = np.random.default_rng(123)

    os.makedirs(out_dir, exist_ok=True)

    for s in seeds:
        if method == "vqc":
            res, hist, _ = run_vqc(
                seed=s,
                iters=iters,
                lr=lr,
                entangler_kind=entangler_kind,
                layers=2,
                out_dir=out_dir,
                record_plots=False,
                use_shots=use_shots,
                shots=shots,
            )
            results.append(res)
            energies.append(hist)
        elif method == "qaoa_p1":
            gamma, beta = qaoa_angles
            val = qaoa_p1_expectation(gamma=gamma, beta=beta)
            res = {
                "seed": s,
                "best_cost": float(val),
                "final_cost_from_probs": float(val),
                "target_mass": 0.0,
                "method": "qaoa_p1",
            }
            results.append(res)
        elif method == "greedy":
            x = greedy_local_search(rng_for_classical)
            v = cut_value(x)
            res = {
                "seed": s,
                "best_cost": float(v),
                "final_cost_from_probs": float(v),
                "target_mass": float(1.0 if v == 5.0 else 0.0),
                "method": "greedy",
            }
            results.append(res)
        elif method == "sa":
            x = simulated_annealing(rng_for_classical)
            v = cut_value(x)
            res = {
                "seed": s,
                "best_cost": float(v),
                "final_cost_from_probs": float(v),
                "target_mass": float(1.0 if v == 5.0 else 0.0),
                "method": "sa",
            }
            results.append(res)
        elif method == "random":
            x = random_cut(rng_for_classical)
            v = cut_value(x)
            res = {
                "seed": s,
                "best_cost": float(v),
                "final_cost_from_probs": float(v),
                "target_mass": float(1.0 if v == 5.0 else 0.0),
                "method": "random",
            }
            results.append(res)
        else:
            raise ValueError(f"Unknown method: {method}")

    # Save raw results
    with open(os.path.join(out_dir, f"{method}_summary.json"), "w") as f:
        json.dump(results, f, indent=2)

    # Aggregate plot for VQC
    if method == "vqc" and len(energies) > 0:
        aggregate_energy_plot(energies, os.path.join(out_dir, f"energy_multi_seed_{entangler_kind}.png"))

    # Print summary with CI
    vals = [r.get("final_cost_from_probs", r.get("best_cost", np.nan)) for r in results]
    lo, hi = ci95(vals)
    mean = float(np.mean(vals))
    std = float(np.std(vals, ddof=1)) if len(vals) > 1 else 0.0
    print(f"[{method}] over {len(results)} seeds: Max-Cut value = {mean:.4f} ± {std:.4f} (95% CI [{lo:.4f},{hi:.4f}])")

    return results


def main():
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(
        description="Hardware-matched VQC for Max-Cut on 5 qubits with baselines and ablations."
    )
    parser.add_argument("--mode", type=str, default="single", choices=["single", "multi"], help="single run or multi-seed")
    parser.add_argument("--seed", type=int, default=42)
    parser.add_argument("--seeds", type=int, nargs="*", default=list(range(1, 21)))
    parser.add_argument("--iters", type=int, default=180)
    parser.add_argument("--lr", type=float, default=0.15)
    parser.add_argument("--entangler", type=str, default="ring", choices=["ring", "all_to_all", "star"])
    parser.add_argument("--layers", type=int, default=2)
    parser.add_argument("--out_dir", type=str, default=".")
    parser.add_argument("--method", type=str, default="vqc", choices=["vqc", "qaoa_p1", "greedy", "sa", "random"])
    parser.add_argument("--qaoa_gamma", type=float, default=0.8)
    parser.add_argument("--qaoa_beta", type=float, default=0.6)
    parser.add_argument("--use_shots", action="store_true", help="Use shot-based energy estimates in VQC training")
    parser.add_argument("--shots", type=int, default=0, help="Number of shots per energy estimate if use_shots is set")
    args = parser.parse_args()

    if args.mode == "single":
        run_experiment(seed=args.seed, iters=args.iters, lr=args.lr, out_dir=args.out_dir, entangler_kind=args.entangler)
    else:
        res = run_multi_seed(
            seeds=args.seeds,
            method=args.method,
            entangler_kind=args.entangler,
            out_dir=args.out_dir,
            iters=args.iters,
            lr=args.lr,
            qaoa_angles=(args.qaoa_gamma, args.qaoa_beta),
            use_shots=args.use_shots,
            shots=args.shots,
        )
        vals = [r.get("final_cost_from_probs", r.get("best_cost", np.nan)) for r in res]
        mean = float(np.mean(vals))
        std = float(np.std(vals, ddof=1)) if len(vals) > 1 else 0.0
        print(f"[{args.method}] over {len(res)} seeds: Max-Cut value = {mean:.4f} ± {std:.4f}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
