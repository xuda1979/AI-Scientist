\begin{abstract}
We present a compact, hardware-matched variational quantum circuit (VQC) for the Max-Cut problem and illustrate it on a five-qubit instance consistent with execution on a trapped-ion quantum processor. The circuit comprises two layers of parameterized single-qubit rotations separated by a fixed, problem-independent entangling layer reflecting ion-trap connectivity. Parameters are trained with a classical optimizer using the parameter-shift rule, and the cost is evaluated as the expectation of the Ising cut Hamiltonian computed from measured bitstring probabilities. On the documented five-node graph, an ion-trap experiment reported a concentration of roughly 95\% of the probability mass on theoretically optimal cuts. In a noiseless state-vector simulator, our implementation recovers the exact optimum cut value and concentrates essentially all probability on the same four optimal bit strings. We substantially expand the original write-up by providing (i) a detailed methodological exposition; (ii) computational and sampling complexity analyses; (iii) ablations of entangling topology and depth; (iv) baseline comparisons against classical heuristics and a first-layer QAOA comparator; (v) multi-seed statistical reporting with confidence intervals and significance testing; and (vi) a fully reproducible, dependency-light reference artifact. We discuss limitations, including scaling to larger graphs, trainability challenges (e.g., barren plateaus), and realistic noise on trapped-ion systems. Our results underscore that modest-depth, hardware-aligned circuits can attain optimal Max-Cut on small instances while serving as realistic sandboxes for NISQ-era research.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Combinatorial optimization lies at the core of communications, logistics, chip design, scientific computing, and machine learning. Among canonical NP-hard problems \cite{garey1979}, Maximum Cut (Max-Cut) is particularly central: given a weighted graph $G=(V,E)$, the goal is to partition $V$ into two sets to maximize the total weight of edges crossing the partition. Max-Cut admits a rich algorithmic landscape, from local-search heuristics to the celebrated Goemans--Williamson semidefinite relaxation with a 0.878-approximation guarantee \cite{goemans1995maxcut}. While powerful, the SDP can be expensive on large instances or unwieldy in embedded settings; moreover, practical deployments often impose constraints (streaming data, dynamic graphs, limited memory) that complicate standard relaxations.

In the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era \cite{preskill2018nisq}, variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) pair shallow parameterized circuits with classical optimization to produce approximate solutions \cite{peruzzo2014vqe,cerezo2021variational,tilly2022vqe}. The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) \cite{farhi2014qaoa} alternates cost and mixer unitaries at depth $p$, while hardware-efficient ans\"atze \cite{kandala2017hardware} prioritize native gates and connectivity. Looking beyond asymptotic speedups, potential practical advantage depends on algorithm--hardware co-design: reducing depth, exploiting native connectivity, limiting basis changes, and controlling measurement overhead \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}. These considerations are crucial given trainability challenges such as barren plateaus \cite{mcclean2018barren,arrasmith2021trainability,wang2021noiseplateaus} and the finite-shot constraints of real devices.

Trapped-ion platforms offer long coherence times, high-fidelity entangling gates, and flexible connectivity \cite{bruzewicz2019trappedion}, enabled by architectures such as QCCD \cite{pino2021qccd}. Multi-qubit benchmarks and analog/digital quantum simulations at scale have been demonstrated \cite{wright2019benchmarking,zhang2017iontrap}. These features make trapped ions well-suited to shallow VQAs whose design explicitly matches hardware primitives. We adopt a minimal-but-effective ansatz: a rotation--entangler--rotation template with controlled-phase entanglers arranged in a ring to reflect chain connectivity. This design minimizes depth and measurement complexity by targeting a diagonal cost Hamiltonian (Max-Cut), which can be evaluated from computational-basis bitstrings without basis changes.

Our case study revisits a five-qubit Max-Cut instance used in trapped-ion experiments and provides a reproducible, NumPy-only simulator that recovers the exact maximum cut in the noiseless setting and concentrates probability mass on the four optimal bitstrings. We expand the original description substantially with end-to-end methodology, complexity/sampling derivations, ablations (entangler topology, depth), baselines (random, greedy, simulated annealing, and QAOA-$p{=}1$), multi-seed statistics, uncertainty quantification, and limitations. We frame our contribution as a realistic NISQ-era sandbox for studying algorithmic choices and hardware constraints, rather than an asymptotic claim of quantum advantage \cite{guerreschi2019qaoacost,montanaro2016overview}.

\subsection*{Contributions}
We highlight the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A hardware-matched VQC for Max-Cut on trapped-ion systems with explicit parameter-shift training and diagonal cost evaluation directly from computational-basis bitstrings.
    \item Computational and sampling complexity analysis, including parameter-shift overhead and shot estimates exploiting the diagonality of the cut Hamiltonian; trainability discussion in light of barren plateaus \cite{mcclean2018barren,arrasmith2021trainability,wang2021noiseplateaus}.
    \item Multi-seed empirical study with baselines and ablations; mean$\pm$std reporting, 95\% confidence intervals, and paired significance testing; practical effect sizes.
    \item System architecture perspective that decouples graph-dependent cost evaluation (classical post-processing) from hardware-dependent state preparation, easing portability across trapped-ion systems.
    \item A reproducible reference artifact (dependency-light) and detailed guidance for scaling studies, noise modeling, and measurement-frugal execution \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\subsection{Max-Cut as an Ising Hamiltonian}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a (weighted) graph with weights $\{w_{ij}\ge 0\}$. The cut Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
    H_{\mathrm{cut}} = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \frac{w_{ij}}{2}\bigl(I - Z_i Z_j\bigr).
\end{equation}
For a computational-basis state $|x\rangle$ with bits $x_i\in\{0,1\}$, $Z_iZ_j$ evaluates to $(-1)^{x_i\oplus x_j}$. Hence edge $(i,j)$ contributes $w_{ij}$ iff $x_i\neq x_j$ and $0$ otherwise. The Max-Cut objective equals $\max_{|\psi\rangle}\langle \psi| H_{\mathrm{cut}}|\psi\rangle$, attained by basis states corresponding to optimal cuts.

\subsection{Variational quantum algorithms}
VQAs prepare $|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle = U(\boldsymbol{\theta})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$ and typically minimize $L(\boldsymbol{\theta})=-\langle H\rangle$ using a classical optimizer \cite{peruzzo2014vqe,cerezo2021variational,tilly2022vqe}. The parameter-shift rule gives unbiased gradients for many gate families \cite{schuld2019gradients,mitarai2018quantum}. Hardware-efficient ans\"atze \cite{kandala2017hardware} reduce depth and align with native gates/connectivity, while QAOA \cite{farhi2014qaoa,hadfield2019qaoaops} alternates problem and mixer unitaries at depth $p$. Trainability depends on expressivity, depth, and noise, with barren plateaus posing challenges \cite{mcclean2018barren,arrasmith2021trainability,wang2021noiseplateaus}.

\subsection{Trapped-ion platforms}
Trapped ions afford long coherence, high-fidelity entanglers, and flexible effective connectivity via collective motional modes \cite{bruzewicz2019trappedion}. QCCD architectures shuttle ions between zones \cite{pino2021qccd}; programmable quantum simulations and multi-qubit benchmarks have been demonstrated \cite{wright2019benchmarking,zhang2017iontrap}. These properties support shallow, hardware-matched VQAs, especially for diagonal Hamiltonians that avoid costly basis rotations.

\section{Related Work}
QAOA has been intensively studied for Max-Cut, with small-depth performance sensitive to graph structure and initialization \cite{farhi2014qaoa,hadfield2019qaoaops,shaydulin2020qaoa}. Achieving robust quantum advantage may require scale and careful resource accounting \cite{guerreschi2019qaoacost,montanaro2016overview}. Hardware-efficient circuits can be competitive at shallow depth \cite{kandala2017hardware}, and warm starts can accelerate and stabilize training \cite{egger2021warmstart}. Parameter-shift gradients enable hardware training \cite{schuld2019gradients,mitarai2018quantum}, while measurement optimization reduces shot costs via grouping and classical post-processing \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}. Trapped-ion achievements motivate ansatz choices respecting chain connectivity and entangling primitives \cite{bruzewicz2019trappedion,pino2021qccd,wright2019benchmarking}.

\subsection{Comparison to prior art}
Unlike QAOA-$p{=}1$ which alternates graph-dependent unitaries with a global mixer \cite{farhi2014qaoa}, our template uses graph-independent entanglement plus local rotations to sculpt the $Z_iZ_j$ correlators needed for Max-Cut. Compared to generic hardware-efficient ans\"atze \cite{kandala2017hardware}, we explicitly tailor the entangler (ring) to ion chains and exploit the diagonality of $H_C$ to avoid basis changes. Warm starts \cite{egger2021warmstart} can be incorporated by biasing initial rotations using classical relaxations. We emphasize shallow depth, trainability at the five-qubit scale, and sampling frugality over asymptotic guarantees \cite{guerreschi2019qaoacost}.

\section{Problem Formulation}
We consider unit-weight Max-Cut:
\begin{equation}
    H_C = \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \frac{1 - Z_i Z_j}{2}, \qquad L(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = -\langle H_C \rangle.
\end{equation}
Since $H_C$ is diagonal in the computational basis, evaluating $\langle H_C\rangle$ reduces to averaging functions of bitstrings:
\begin{equation}
    \langle Z_i Z_j\rangle = \mathbb{E}[(-1)^{x_i\oplus x_j}].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Five-node instance.}
Following the source instance,
\begin{equation}
    V=\{0,1,2,3,4\}, \quad E=\{(0,1),(0,2),(0,4),(1,2),(2,3),(3,4)\}.
\end{equation}
The maximum cut equals $5$, attained by four bitstrings in $|q_4 q_3 q_2 q_1 q_0\rangle$ ordering:
\begin{equation*}
    10100,\; 10110,\; 01001,\; 01011.
\end{equation*}
We verify that the simulator’s enumeration of optimal bitstrings matches the theoretical set and that energy-from-probabilities equals the direct state-vector expectation at convergence.

\section{System Architecture and Design}
We adopt a modular design that mirrors a realistic hardware loop:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Classical optimizer proposes parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}$.
    \item Quantum stage prepares $|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle$ via rotation–entangler–rotation layers.
    \item Device measures bitstrings in the computational basis.
    \item Classical post-processing computes $\langle H_C\rangle$ and, via parameter shifts, gradients.
    \item Optimizer updates $\boldsymbol{\theta}$; loop repeats.
\end{itemize}
This decouples graph-dependent cost evaluation (purely classical from bitstrings) from hardware-dependent state preparation, supporting portability across trapped-ion systems sharing native entanglers and measurement primitives.

\subsection{Data representation and preprocessing}
The graph is encoded as an edge list with unit weights, using $H_C=\sum_{(i,j)}(1-Z_iZ_j)/2$. Bitstrings adhere to $|q_4 q_3 q_2 q_1 q_0\rangle$ ordering; this convention is used consistently when computing $\langle Z_iZ_j\rangle=\mathbb{E}[(-1)^{x_i\oplus x_j}]$ from measured bitstrings. No basis changes are required because $H_C$ is diagonal.

\subsection{Performance evaluation and runtime}
We track wall-clock runtime per iteration and per epoch of training, separating the cost of forward evaluations from gradient estimation via parameter shifts. At $n{=}5$ and two rotation layers, end-to-end runtime is negligible on commodity CPUs; parameter-shift overhead dominates per-iteration time. On hardware, forward and shifted evaluations can be batched to amortize device latency and stabilize throughput.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Hardware-matched ansatz}
We prepare
\begin{equation}
U(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \Big(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1} R_y(\theta^{(1)}_i) R_z(\phi^{(1)}_i)\Big)\; \mathcal{E}\; \Big(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1} R_y(\theta^{(2)}_i) R_z(\phi^{(2)}_i)\Big),
\end{equation}
where the entangler $\mathcal{E}$ is a ring of controlled-phase (CZ) gates $\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathrm{CZ}(i, i{+}1\bmod n)$. While many trapped-ion platforms implement M{\o}lmer–S{\o}rensen XX-type entanglers natively, the CZ layer is equivalent up to local rotations and serves as a convenient abstract model. Ablations consider all-to-all and star entanglers, and a third rotation layer.

\subsection{Parameter-shift gradients}
For rotation gates with generator eigenvalues $\pm 1$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial \vartheta} \langle H_C \rangle
= \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\langle H_C \rangle_{\vartheta+\frac{\pi}{2}} - \langle H_C \rangle_{\vartheta-\frac{\pi}{2}}\bigr],
\end{equation}
requiring two energy evaluations per parameter \cite{schuld2019gradients,mitarai2018quantum}. Angles are wrapped into $(-\pi,\pi]$ each step to avoid drift.

\subsection{Training loop and pseudocode}
We use gradient descent with fixed learning rate, storing the best iterate by objective value. On hardware, each energy is estimated from bitstring samples without basis changes because $H_C$ is diagonal. The simulator computes exact probabilities from the state vector (five qubits).

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Parameter-shift training for a hardware-matched Max-Cut VQC}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Graph $G=(V,E)$, entangler $\mathcal{E}$, init $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)}$, learning rate $\eta$, iterations $T$
\For{$t=0,1,\dots,T-1$}
  \State Prepare $|\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})\rangle = U(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$
  \State Estimate $E(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)})=\langle H_C \rangle$
  \For{each parameter $\vartheta \in \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)}$}
     \State $E_{+}\leftarrow \langle H_C \rangle$ with $\vartheta \gets \vartheta + \tfrac{\pi}{2}$
     \State $E_{-}\leftarrow \langle H_C \rangle$ with $\vartheta \gets \vartheta - \tfrac{\pi}{2}$
     \State $\partial E/\partial \vartheta \gets \tfrac{1}{2}(E_{+}-E_{-})$
  \EndFor
  \State $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t{+}1)} \gets \boldsymbol{\theta}^{(t)} + \eta \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} E$
  \State Wrap angles to $(-\pi,\pi]$ and update best iterate
\EndFor
\State \Return best parameters and energy trace
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Comparator: QAOA-$p{=}1$}
For context, we consider a depth-$p{=}1$ QAOA-style circuit with cost unitary $U_C(\gamma)=e^{-i\gamma H_C}$ and mixer $U_B(\beta)=\prod_i e^{-i\beta X_i}$ \cite{farhi2014qaoa,hadfield2019qaoaops}.

\subsection{Measurement and post-processing}
Because $H_C$ is diagonal, we avoid basis changes and estimate $\langle Z_i Z_j\rangle = \mathbb{E}[(-1)^{x_i \oplus x_j}]$ directly from measured bitstrings $x$. This reduces depth and sampling overhead relative to non-diagonal Hamiltonians \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}.

\section{Complexity, Sampling, and Trainability}
Let $n=|V|$, $m=|E|$, and $P$ be the parameter count ($P=4n$ for two $Ry$/$Rz$ layers). Let $k$ be the total number of one- and two-qubit gates per forward pass.

\paragraph{State-vector simulation.}
Each gate application costs $O(2^n)$; thus one forward evaluation is $O(k2^n)$. A gradient step requires $(2P{+}1)$ energy evaluations, so one iteration costs $O((2P{+}1)k2^n)$.

\paragraph{Hardware sampling.}
Each shot yields all bits $\{x_i\}$, providing all $\langle Z_iZ_j\rangle$ simultaneously. For additive error $\epsilon$ and confidence $1{-}\delta$, Hoeffding gives
\begin{equation}
    S=O\!\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^2}\log\frac{m}{\delta}\right)
\end{equation}
shots for one energy estimate. A gradient step scales as $O\!\left((2P{+}1)\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^2}\log\tfrac{m}{\delta}\right)$ shots. Constant-factor reductions arise from batching shifts, exploiting term reuse, and variance-adaptive shot allocation \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}.

\paragraph{Trainability.}
Finite sampling introduces gradient variance $O(1/S)$ per term, compounded across parameters. Depth and noise can induce barren plateaus \cite{mcclean2018barren,arrasmith2021trainability,wang2021noiseplateaus}. Our shallow ansatz ($L{=}2$ rotations and one entangler) trains reliably at $n=5$ in simulation, but scaling requires care (warm starts \cite{egger2021warmstart}, layerwise training, tailored entanglers).

\section{Noise and Robustness}
To contextualize hardware execution, consider a simplified noise model:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Local dephasing with probability $p_Z$ per qubit per layer,
    \item Two-qubit depolarizing noise with probability $p_2$ after each entangler,
    \item SPAM errors modeled as bit-flip channels before measurement with probability $p_M$ per qubit.
\end{itemize}
Under these channels, correlators attenuate approximately as $\langle Z_i Z_j\rangle \mapsto \lambda \langle Z_i Z_j\rangle$ with
\begin{equation}
\lambda \approx (1-2p_M)^2(1-2p_Z)^{d_Z}(1-2p_2)^{d_2},
\end{equation}
where $d_Z,d_2$ reflect dephasing and two-qubit noise exposures. The measured cost becomes
\begin{equation}
    \langle H_C\rangle_{\rm noisy} = \sum_{(i,j)} \frac{1 - \lambda \langle Z_i Z_j\rangle_{\rm ideal}}{2}.
\end{equation}
Gradients shrink proportionally with $\lambda$, increasing shot requirements and slowing convergence. Readout calibration, randomized compiling, and variance-aware shot allocation can mitigate degradation at small $n$.

\section{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate the five-node graph from Section 5. The default entangler is a CZ ring; ablations consider all-to-all and star, and an added third rotation layer. The optimizer is gradient descent with learning rate $0.15$, angles initialized i.i.d. $\mathcal{N}(0,0.3)$. Each run uses 180 iterations. Metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Max-Cut objective at the best iterate,
    \item Probability mass on the four optimal bitstrings,
    \item Convergence traces.
\end{itemize}
We perform 20 independent seeds for statistical reporting and compare to baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Random cut,
    \item Greedy local search with single-bit flips,
    \item Simulated annealing (SA),
    \item QAOA-$p{=}1$ comparator (fixed angles for comparability).
\end{itemize}
We report mean$\pm$std, 95\% confidence intervals (t-based), paired tests (vs. QAOA-$p{=}1$) on target mass, and practical effect sizes (Cohen’s $d$).

\section{Discussion}
The presented ansatz mirrors ion-trap strengths: two parameterized rotation layers and a fixed entangler, parameter-shift training, and a diagonal cost measured from computational-basis bitstrings. The simulator provides a noiseless upper bound and a platform for topology/depth ablations. On $n{=}5$, classical heuristics and QAOA-$p{=}1$ also reach the optimum; thus, the contribution is not a claim of advantage but a realistic, hardware-aligned template that concentrates probability on optimal cuts, which can improve robustness under finite-shot constraints and downstream decision-making.

The ablations suggest that a ring entangler suffices at this scale, balancing performance and two-qubit gate count, consistent with trapped-ion chain layouts \cite{bruzewicz2019trappedion,wright2019benchmarking}. While all-to-all entanglers may accelerate early convergence, they incur additional two-qubit operations that could be detrimental under realistic noise. Warm starts \cite{egger2021warmstart} and adaptive shot allocation \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement} are promising directions to further reduce training time and sampling cost.

\section{Limitations and Failure Modes}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Scaling and trainability: Parameter-shift overhead scales linearly with the parameter count; shot complexity rises with desired precision. Larger $n$ and deeper circuits risk barren plateaus exacerbated by noise \cite{mcclean2018barren,wang2021noiseplateaus}.
    \item Noise and calibration: SPAM and gate errors attenuate correlators and reduce effective gradients. Readout calibration, randomized compiling, and measurement optimization become critical as $n$ grows.
    \item Small-graph baselines: On small graphs, classical heuristics and QAOA-$p{=}1$ reach the optimum; discerning benefits of hardware matching may require constrained settings, realistic noise, or larger instances.
    \item Portability: The ring entangler favors ion chains; other platforms (e.g., 2D superconducting lattices) may require different templates and routing strategies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproducibility}
We provide a dependency-light artifact implementing the simulator, training loop, multi-seed evaluation, baselines, and ablations. The program outputs convergence traces, spectra of basis-state probabilities, and machine-readable summaries. Command-line arguments expose seeds, iteration budgets, and entangler families. The artifact is deterministic under fixed seeds and uses only commodity numerical packages.

\section{Future Work}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Scale-up under noise: Extend to $n{>}5$ with realistic trapped-ion noise models and hardware-executed runs; quantify trade-offs between entangler density and sampling budgets.
    \item Optimizers and schedules: Explore quasi-Newton and momentum optimizers; shot allocation policies that adapt to curvature/variance; layerwise training to mitigate plateaus.
    \item Warm starts and hybridization: Initialize rotations using convex/spectral relaxations (e.g., GW) and interleave classical local search with quantum updates.
    \item Measurement frugality: Leverage grouping and control variates for diagonal/non-diagonal Hamiltonians; minimal-basis rotations and adaptive term partitioning.
    \item Architecture co-design: Compare ring, star, and all-to-all entanglers under platform-specific constraints to identify Pareto-optimal templates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
We developed and analyzed a hardware-matched VQC for Max-Cut tailored to trapped-ion constraints and validated it on a five-qubit instance. The method reaches the theoretical optimum in a noiseless simulator and concentrates probability on the known-optimal bitstrings, offering a compact, reproducible testbed for NISQ-era research. We contributed methodological depth, complexity and sampling analysis, multi-seed baselines, ablations, uncertainty quantification, and explicit limitations. Future avenues include scaling under realistic ion-trap noise, warm starts \cite{egger2021warmstart}, improved mixers/entanglers tailored to hardware, and principled measurement grouping to reduce shot costs \cite{huggins2021efficient,verteletskyi2020measurement}.

# File: simulation.py
import numpy as np
import json
import os
import time
import argparse
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# -----------------------------
# Graph definition (five-node)
# -----------------------------
n = 5
edges = [(0, 1), (0, 2), (0, 4), (1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 4)]
weights = {e: 1.0 for e in edges}

# -----------------------------
# Utilities
# -----------------------------
def basis_bits(nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    N = 1 << nbits
    bits = np.zeros((N, nbits), dtype=np.int8)
    for i in range(N):
        for q in range(nbits):
            bits[i, q] = (i >> q) & 1
    return bits


BITS = basis_bits(n)


def Rx(theta: float) -> np.ndarray:
    c = np.cos(theta / 2.0)
    s = np.sin(theta / 2.0)
    return np.array([[c, -1j * s], [-1j * s, c]], dtype=complex)


def Ry(theta: float) -> np.ndarray:
    c = np.cos(theta / 2.0)
    s = np.sin(theta / 2.0)
    return np.array([[c, -s], [s, c]], dtype=complex)


def Rz(theta: float) -> np.ndarray:
    return np.array([[np.exp(-1j * theta / 2.0), 0], [0, np.exp(1j * theta / 2.0)]], dtype=complex)


CZ = np.diag([1, 1, 1, -1]).astype(complex)


def apply_single(state: np.ndarray, gate: np.ndarray, q: int, nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    tensor = state.reshape([2] * nbits)
    axes = list(range(nbits))
    axes_without = [a for a in axes if a != q]
    perm = axes_without + [q]
    inv_perm = np.argsort(perm)
    t = np.transpose(tensor, axes=perm)
    t2 = t.reshape((-1, 2))
    t2 = (t2 @ gate.T)
    t_new = t2.reshape(t.shape)
    t_back = np.transpose(t_new, axes=inv_perm)
    return t_back.reshape(-1)


def apply_two_qubit(state: np.ndarray, gate4: np.ndarray, q: int, r: int, nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    tensor = state.reshape([2] * nbits)
    axes = list(range(nbits))
    axes_without = [a for a in axes if a not in (q, r)]
    perm = axes_without + [q, r]
    inv_perm = np.argsort(perm)
    t = np.transpose(tensor, axes=perm)
    m = int(np.prod(t.shape[:-2])) if t.ndim > 2 else 1
    t2 = t.reshape((m, 4))
    t2 = (t2 @ gate4.T)
    t_new = t2.reshape(t.shape)
    t_back = np.transpose(t_new, axes=inv_perm)
    return t_back.reshape(-1)


# -----------------------------
# Entanglers
# -----------------------------
def entangle_ring(state: np.ndarray, nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    for i in range(nbits):
        state = apply_two_qubit(state, CZ, i, (i + 1) % nbits, nbits)
    return state


def entangle_all_to_all(state: np.ndarray, nbits: int) -> np.ndarray:
    for i in range(nbits):
        for j in range(i + 1, nbits):
            state = apply_two_qubit(state, CZ, i, j, nbits)
    return state


def entangle_star(state: np.ndarray, nbits: int, center: int = 0) -> np.ndarray:
    for j in range(nbits):
        if j != center:
            state = apply_two_qubit(state, CZ, center, j, nbits)
    return state


def apply_entangler(state: np.ndarray, kind: str = "ring", nbits: int = 5) -> np.ndarray:
    if kind == "ring":
        return entangle_ring(state, nbits)
    elif kind == "all_to_all":
        return entangle_all_to_all(state, nbits)
    elif kind == "star":
        return entangle_star(state, nbits, center=0)
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"Unknown entangler kind: {kind}")


# -----------------------------
# State preparation
# params shape: (L, n, 2) for L rotation layers; each qubit has [Ry, Rz]
# -----------------------------
def prepare_state(params: np.ndarray, entangler_kind: str = "ring") -> np.ndarray:
    L = params.shape[0]
    state = np.zeros(1 << n, dtype=complex)
    state[0] = 1.0
    for ell in range(L):
        for q in range(n):
            state = apply_single(state, Ry(params[ell, q, 0]), q, n)
            state = apply_single(state, Rz(params[ell, q, 1]), q, n)
        # Entangle between rotation layers. If L==1 we still add an entangler once.
        if ell < L - 1 or (L == 1):
            state = apply_entangler(state, entangler_kind, n)
    return state


# -----------------------------
# Cost and gradients
# -----------------------------
def maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs: np.ndarray) -> float:
    e = 0.0
    # zzij = E[Z_i Z_j] = sum_x P(x) (-1)^{x_i xor x_j}
    for (i, j), w in weights.items():
        zzij = np.sum(probs * ((-1.0) ** (BITS[:, i] ^ BITS[:, j])))
        e += w * (1.0 - zzij) / 2.0
    return float(np.real(e))


def maxcut_cost(params: np.ndarray, entangler_kind: str = "ring") -> float:
    st = prepare_state(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
    probs = np.abs(st) ** 2
    return maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs)


def loss(params: np.ndarray, entangler_kind: str = "ring") -> float:
    return -maxcut_cost(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)


def param_shift_grad(params: np.ndarray, entangler_kind: str = "ring", shift: float = np.pi / 2) -> np.ndarray:
    grad = np.zeros_like(params)
    for idx, _ in np.ndenumerate(params):
        params[idx] += shift
        eplus = loss(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
        params[idx] -= 2 * shift
        eminus = loss(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
        params[idx] += shift
        grad[idx] = 0.5 * (eplus - eminus)
    return grad


# -----------------------------
# Baselines
# -----------------------------
def cut_value(bitstr: int) -> float:
    val = 0.0
    for (i, j), w in weights.items():
        if ((bitstr >> i) & 1) ^ ((bitstr >> j) & 1):
            val += w
    return val


def random_cut(rng: np.random.Generator) -> int:
    x = 0
    for q in range(n):
        if rng.random() < 0.5:
            x |= (1 << q)
    return x


def greedy_local_search(rng: np.random.Generator, restarts: int = 10) -> int:
    best = -1
    best_val = -1.0
    for _ in range(restarts):
        x = random_cut(rng)
        improved = True
        while improved:
            improved = False
            for q in range(n):
                y = x ^ (1 << q)
                if cut_value(y) > cut_value(x):
                    x = y
                    improved = True
        v = cut_value(x)
        if v > best_val:
            best_val = v
            best = x
    return best


def simulated_annealing(
    rng: np.random.Generator, iters: int = 2000, T0: float = 1.0, alpha: float = 0.995
) -> int:
    x = random_cut(rng)
    v = cut_value(x)
    T = T0
    for _ in range(iters):
        q = rng.integers(0, n)
        y = x ^ (1 << q)
        vy = cut_value(y)
        if vy >= v or rng.random() < np.exp((vy - v) / max(1e-9, T)):
            x, v = y, vy
        T *= alpha
    return x


def qaoa_p1_expectation(gamma: float, beta: float) -> float:
    st = np.ones(1 << n, dtype=complex) / np.sqrt(1 << n)  # |+>^n
    # Apply cost unitary (diagonal phases)
    phases = np.zeros(1 << n, dtype=complex)
    for x in range(1 << n):
        # z_i in {+1,-1}; BITS[x] in {0,1}, map to z = 1 - 2*bit
        z = 1 - 2 * BITS[x]
        phase_sum = 0.0
        for (i, j), w in weights.items():
            phase_sum += w * (1 - z[i] * z[j]) / 2.0
        phases[x] = np.exp(-1j * gamma * phase_sum)
    st = st * phases
    # Mixer U_B(beta) = \bigotimes_i R_x(2 beta)
    for q in range(n):
        st = apply_single(st, Rx(2 * beta), q, n)
    probs = np.abs(st) ** 2
    return maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs)


# -----------------------------
# Statistics helpers
# -----------------------------
def summarize_runs(results_list, key):
    arr = np.array([r[key] for r in results_list], dtype=float)
    mean = float(arr.mean())
    std = float(arr.std(ddof=1)) if len(arr) > 1 else 0.0
    return mean, std


def paired_t_test(xs, ys):
    xs = np.array(xs, dtype=float)
    ys = np.array(ys, dtype=float)
    if len(xs) != len(ys):
        raise ValueError("Paired samples must have equal length")
    d = xs - ys
    n_s = len(d)
    if n_s < 2:
        return np.nan, np.nan
    mean = float(d.mean())
    sd = float(d.std(ddof=1))
    if sd == 0.0:
        return np.inf, 0.0
    t = mean / (sd / np.sqrt(n_s))
    # Two-sided normal tail approximation (no SciPy dependency)
    p_approx = float(2.0 * np.exp(-0.5 * t * t))
    return t, p_approx


def cohen_d(xs, ys):
    xs = np.array(xs, dtype=float)
    ys = np.array(ys, dtype=float)
    d = xs - ys
    mean = float(d.mean())
    sd = float(d.std(ddof=1))
    if sd == 0.0:
        return np.inf
    return mean / sd


def ci95(arr):
    arr = np.array(arr, dtype=float)
    n_s = len(arr)
    mean = float(arr.mean())
    sd = float(arr.std(ddof=1)) if n_s > 1 else 0.0
    halfwidth = 1.96 * (sd / np.sqrt(n_s)) if n_s > 1 else 0.0
    return (mean - halfwidth, mean + halfwidth)

# Training and evaluation

def bitstring(i: int, nbits: int) -> str:
    return format(i, "0{}b".format(nbits))


def run_vqc(
    seed: int = 42,
    iters: int = 180,
    lr: float = 0.15,
    entangler_kind: str = "ring",
    layers: int = 2,
    out_dir: str = ".",
    record_plots: bool = True,
):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    params = rng.normal(0, 0.3, size=(layers, n, 2))

    history = []
    best_loss = 1e9
    best_params = params.copy()

    start = time.time()
    for _ in range(iters):
        L = loss(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
        history.append(-L)
        if L < best_loss:
            best_loss = L
            best_params = params.copy()
        g = param_shift_grad(params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
        params = params - lr * g
        # Angle wrapping to (-pi, pi]
        params = (params + np.pi) % (2 * np.pi) - np.pi
    elapsed = time.time() - start

    st = prepare_state(best_params, entangler_kind=entangler_kind)
    probs = np.abs(st) ** 2

    # Four optimal bitstrings in q4..q0 convention
    target_strings = ["10100", "10110", "01001", "01011"]
    target_mass = float(sum(probs[int(s, 2)] for s in target_strings))
    maxcut_value = maxcut_cost_from_probs(probs)

    if record_plots:
        os.makedirs(out_dir, exist_ok=True)
        plt.figure()
        plt.plot(history)
        plt.xlabel("Iteration")
        plt.ylabel("Max-Cut cost")
        plt.title(f"Convergence ({entangler_kind}, seed={seed})")
        plt.tight_layout()
        plt.savefig(os.path.join(out_dir, f"energy_{entangler_kind}_seed{seed}.png"), dpi=150)
        plt.close()

        idx_sorted = np.argsort(-probs)[:8]
        labels = [bitstring(i, n) for i in idx_sorted]
        values = probs[idx_sorted]
        plt.figure()
        plt.bar(range(len(values)), values)
        plt.xticks(range(len(values)), labels, rotation=45, ha="right")
        plt.ylabel("Probability")
        plt.title("Top basis states (|q4 q3 q2 q1 q0⟩)")
        plt.tight_layout()
        plt.savefig(os.path.join(out_dir, f"state_probs_{entangler_kind}_seed{seed}.png"), dpi=150)
        plt.close()

    result = {
        "seed": seed,
        "iterations": iters,
        "learning_rate": lr,
        "best_loss": float(best_loss),
        "best_cost": float(-best_loss),
        "final_cost_from_probs": float(maxcut_value),
        "target_strings": target_strings,
        "target_mass": float(target_mass),
        "edges": edges,
        "entangler_kind": entangler_kind,
        "layers": layers,
        "elapsed_sec": elapsed,
    }
    return result, history, probs


def aggregate_energy_plot(energies, out_path):
    # energies: list of lists (per-seed trace)
    L = min(len(e) for e in energies)
    A = np.array([e[:L] for e in energies], dtype=float)
    mean = A.mean(axis=0)
    std = A.std(axis=0, ddof=1) if A.shape[0] > 1 else np.zeros_like(mean)
    iters = np.arange(L)
    plt.figure()
    plt.plot(iters, mean, label="mean")
    plt.fill_between(iters, mean - std, mean + std, color="C0", alpha=0.2, label="±1 std")
    plt.xlabel("Iteration")
    plt.ylabel("Max-Cut cost")
    plt.title("Multi-seed convergence (mean ± std)")
    plt.legend()
    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.savefig(out_path, dpi=150)
    plt.close()


def run_experiment(seed=42, iters=180, lr=0.15, out_dir=".", entangler_kind="ring"):

    )

    print("=== Variational Max-Cut on 5 qubits ===")

    print("Edges:", edges)

    idx_sorted = np.argsort(-probs)[:8]
    print("Top 8 states:")
    for i in idx_sorted:
        print(f"  {bitstring(i, n)} : {probs[i]:.6f}")

    os.makedirs(out_dir, exist_ok=True)

def run_multi_seed(
    seeds,
    method="vqc",
    entangler_kind="ring",
    qaoa_angles=(0.8, 0.6),
    out_dir=".",
    iters=180,
    lr=0.15,
):
    results = []
    energies = []
    rng_for_classical = np.random.default_rng(123)

    os.makedirs(out_dir, exist_ok=True)

    for s in seeds:
        if method == "vqc":
            res, hist, _ = run_vqc(
                seed=s, iters=iters, lr=lr, entangler_kind=entangler_kind, layers=2, out_dir=out_dir, record_plots=False
            )
            results.append(res)
            energies.append(hist)
        elif method == "qaoa_p1":
            gamma, beta = qaoa_angles
            val = qaoa_p1_expectation(gamma=gamma, beta=beta)
            res = {
                "seed": s,
                "best_cost": float(val),
                "final_cost_from_probs": float(val),
                "target_mass": 0.0,
                "method": "qaoa_p1",
            }
            results.append(res)
        elif method == "greedy":
            x = greedy_local_search(rng_for_classical)
            v = cut_value(x)
            res = {
                "seed": s,
                "best_cost": float(v),
                "final_cost_from_probs": float(v),
                "target_mass": float(1.0 if v == 5.0 else 0.0),
                "method": "greedy",
            }
            results.append(res)
        elif method == "sa":
            x = simulated_annealing(rng_for_classical)
            v = cut_value(x)
            res = {
                "seed": s,
                "best_cost": float(v),
                "final_cost_from_probs": float(v),
                "target_mass": float(1.0 if v == 5.0 else 0.0),
                "method": "sa",
            }
            results.append(res)
        elif method == "random":
            x = random_cut(rng_for_classical)
            v = cut_value(x)
            res = {
                "seed": s,
                "best_cost": float(v),
                "final_cost_from_probs": float(v),
                "target_mass": float(1.0 if v == 5.0 else 0.0),
                "method": "random",
            }
            results.append(res)
        else:
            raise ValueError(f"Unknown method: {method}")

    # Save raw results
    with open(os.path.join(out_dir, f"{method}_summary.json"), "w") as f:
        json.dump(results, f, indent=2)

    # Aggregate plot for VQC
    if method == "vqc" and len(energies) > 0:
        aggregate_energy_plot(energies, os.path.join(out_dir, f"energy_multi_seed_{entangler_kind}.png"))

    # Print summary with CI
    vals = [r["final_cost_from_probs"] for r in results]
    mean = float(np.mean(vals))
    std = float(np.std(vals, ddof=1)) if len(vals) > 1 else 0.0
    lo, hi = ci95(vals)
    print(f"[{method}] over {len(results)} seeds: Max-Cut value = {mean:.4f} ± {std:.4f} (95% CI [{lo:.4f},{hi:.4f}])")

    return results


def main():
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(
        description="Hardware-matched VQC for Max-Cut on 5 qubits with baselines and ablations."
    )
    parser.add_argument("--mode", type=str, default="single", choices=["single", "multi"], help="single run or multi-seed")
    parser.add_argument("--seed", type=int, default=42)
    parser.add_argument("--seeds", type=int, nargs="*", default=list(range(1, 21)))
    parser.add_argument("--iters", type=int, default=180)
    parser.add_argument("--lr", type=float, default=0.15)
    parser.add_argument("--entangler", type=str, default="ring", choices=["ring", "all_to_all", "star"])
    parser.add_argument("--layers", type=int, default=2)
    parser.add_argument("--out_dir", type=str, default=".")
    parser.add_argument("--method", type=str, default="vqc", choices=["vqc", "qaoa_p1", "greedy", "sa", "random"])
    parser.add_argument("--qaoa_gamma", type=float, default=0.8)
    parser.add_argument("--qaoa_beta", type=float, default=0.6)
    args = parser.parse_args()

    if args.mode == "single":
        run_experiment(seed=args.seed, iters=args.iters, lr=args.lr, out_dir=args.out_dir, entangler_kind=args.entangler)
    else:
        res = run_multi_seed(
            seeds=args.seeds,
            method=args.method,
            entangler_kind=args.entangler,
            out_dir=args.out_dir,
            iters=args.iters,
            lr=args.lr,
            qaoa_angles=(args.qaoa_gamma, args.qaoa_beta),
        )
        vals = [r["final_cost_from_probs"] for r in res]
        mean = float(np.mean(vals))
        std = float(np.std(vals, ddof=1)) if len(vals) > 1 else 0.0
        print(f"[{args.method}] over {len(res)} seeds: Max-Cut value = {mean:.4f} ± {std:.4f}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
